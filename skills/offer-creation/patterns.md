# Patterns: every offer-creation technique, deduplicated across the 10 books

Each entry: **Pattern** (books) — what to do → when → why. Where books overlap, the first-listed book owns the name.

## A. Discover the job and the segment

1. **Write the job as progress + circumstance** (08, 07, 02) — "Help me [progress] when [circumstance]", with functional, social and emotional dimensions → before any offer, outreach or page → stable across solutions; "automate intake" is a product, not a job.
2. **Timeline interview** (07, 09) — walk a recent buyer or "firer" backwards: first thought, passive looking, active looking, deciding, onboarding, use; capture pushes, pulls, anxieties, habits, trade-offs in their words → first discovery experiment for any new segment → reconstructs cause; hypotheticals produce politeness.
3. **Hunt the struggling moment** (07) — the event where they said "I can't keep doing this" → feed it to headlines, cold openers, LinkedIn posts → demand comes from the push, not your pull.
4. **Look where current solutions fail** (08, 10) — non-consumers, workarounds (nephew's Zapier, WhatsApp groups), negative jobs, second-tier noncustomers who rejected "AI" → when choosing a segment or a Create factor → surveys of current buyers never show these.
5. **Score the starving crowd** (01) — pain, purchasing power, easy to target, growing → when picking the first industry → a perfect offer to an unreachable or broke market yields nothing.
6. **Rank jobs, pains, gains** (02) — importance, severity, relevance; design only for the top → when turning interviews into a profile → unranked profiles justify every deliverable.
7. **Describe best-fit customers by traits that predict caring** (03, 02) — "owner handles leads personally", "already paying a VA", "multi-location" → in positioning, targeting lists, qualification → firmographics predict who, not why.

## B. Position against what the buyer would really do

8. **List true competitive alternatives** (03, 08, 10) — do nothing, hire a VA, DIY Zapier, keep the habit; not rival agencies → first step of positioning; also the "unlike" clause (02) → the buyer compares you to these whatever you say.
9. **Unique attributes → value themes → proof** (03, 05) — only attributes the alternatives lack; cluster into 2–4 themes; attach proof to each → positioning document, website sections → attributes without value are features; value without proof is a claim.
10. **Pick the small pond** (03, 01) — Big Fish, Small Pond over Create a New Game; name the sub-segment in the category line → when writing "what we are" → a sub-segment has a budget line and vocabulary; a new category needs years of teaching.
11. **Draw the strategy canvas and ERRC grid** (10) — plot incumbents' and alternatives' value curves; Eliminate, Reduce, Raise, Create; check focus, divergence, tagline → when your offer reads like every other AI agency → cuts fund the raises; a flat "better at everything" curve is a price fight.
12. **Treat habit as the incumbent's advantage** (07, 03) — show the switching path (parallel run, same channels, no retraining) → when "do nothing" is winning → the current way works well enough or they would have moved.
13. **Trend as "why now", never as category** (03) — "patients expect instant replies" explains timing; "AI-powered" is not a category → hero copy and proposals → buyers must know what you are before they care why now.

## C. Build the offer

14. **Value Equation audit** (01) — Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood ÷ Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice; attack the denominator first → when an offer feels weak and you are tempted to discount → done-for-you and speed are where AI beats a VA.
15. **Problems → solutions → delivery vehicles → trim → stack** (01, 02) — exhaustive problem list (including problems your solution creates), each rewritten as a solution, each given a vehicle, then keep high-value/low-cost → when you have the job and need deliverables → starting from capabilities produces a feature list.
16. **Job spec as the brief** (08) — dimensions, circumstance, trade-offs, obstacles, required experiences, definition of done → before building; check every deliverable against it → keeps you from feature drift.
17. **Leaders, fillers, killers** (04) — build around the leader, tier with fillers, remove or make optional any killer → when deciding what goes in the bundle → one killer lowers WTP for everything.
18. **Design the little hires** (07, 08) — day-one and week-one wins (daily digest, first booked lead untouched) → onboarding section of every offer → the system is fired silently if use fails, however good the sale.
19. **Reduce anxiety with structure** (07, 01) — shadow/approval mode, reversible step, conditional guarantee, monitoring alert, reference call → when a prospect stalls or in the offer's risk section → reassurance does not shrink anxiety; structure does.
20. **Conditional guarantee on a metric you control** (01, 05) — reply time, hours removed, go-live date; keep-working-until or next-month-free; stack with a short unconditional window → every offer → raises perceived likelihood and signals you expect to deliver; never guarantee the client's revenue.
21. **Honest scarcity and event-tied urgency** (01) — real build slots per month; case-study rate ending → offer page and proposal terms → fake deadlines destroy the believability the guarantee bought.
22. **Bonus instead of discount** (01, 04) — name it, link it to a problem, give proof, value it → when pressed on price → a discount lowers every future anchor; a bonus raises value.
23. **MAGIC name** (01) — Magnetic reason, Avatar, Goal, Interval, Container → the headline and the proposal title → a named, time-bound container is referable and priceable.
24. **Touchstone and Big Four audit** (05) — one sentence that answers "what are you selling, how much, why believe you, what's in it for me" within three seconds → first line of page, cold message, proposal cover → unanswered questions become silent objections.
25. **Believability stack** (05, 03) — demonstration on their own data, factual proof, social proof, credibility, guarantee; put proof next to each claim → under the hero line; in the proposal → claims without adjacent proof are discounted to zero.
26. **Core offer before intensifiers** (05, 01) — make the offer irresistible on value, then add bonuses and urgency → ordering rule for every page → intensifiers cannot rescue a weak core.

## D. Price

27. **WTP talk in discovery** (04, 06) — acceptable / expensive / prohibitive; purchase probability at a price; most-least on features → before scoping or building → the answer changes what to build, not just the price.
28. **Outside-in business case** (04, 10) — leads lost × conversion × value × margin; hours × loaded cost → the value section of every proposal → price seen before its value is always too high.
29. **Good-better-best with the target in the middle** (04, 06) — three options, value metric the buyer counts (locations, leads), top option as anchor → every proposal → turns yes/no into which.
30. **Price integrity** (04, 06) — concede scope, never price; never hourly; decide maximization vs penetration explicitly → when pushed → discounting teaches the real price.
31. **Price corridor of the mass** (10, 04) — price between the cheaper alternative (VA, DIY) and the expensive one (full-time hire, enterprise consultancy) → setting the headline price → utility → price → cost, never cost-plus.
32. **Paid diagnostic first** (06) — short audit with written recommendation and options, credited against the build → when asked for a proposal or free scoping → free thinking is discounted; paying buyers are qualified buyers.
33. **Money early, expert posture** (06) — ask cost of the problem and budget range in the first conversations; lead the next step; do not chase → every sales conversation → the one who leads is the expert.

## E. Test before building

34. **Riskiest hypothesis first; D/F/V tagging** (09, 02) — for a services offer, desirability and viability dominate → planning the test sequence → feasibility tests feel productive and prove nothing commercial.
35. **Evidence ladder** (09, 04) — say < intend < click < pay; build decisions need "pay" → choosing the next experiment → interviews are not validation.
36. **Discovery → validation sequence** (09) — interviews → landing page with Touchstone and prices → mock sale / deposit → concierge or Wizard of Oz pilot → build → launching any new offer → each step sharpens before the next spends.
37. **Test Card / Learning Card** (02, 09) — criterion as a count decided in advance; observation, insight, decision after → every experiment → defence against post-hoc success.
38. **Paid concierge pilot** (09, 06) — deliver the outcome semi-manually at full fee for 14 days before automating → first two clients of a new offer → strongest evidence at lowest build cost, and preserves the expert posture.
39. **Four-failure post-mortem** (04) — Feature Shock, Minivation, Hidden Gem, Undead → when an offer is not selling → names the fix: cut, raise price and ambition, spin out, kill.

## Where the books disagree
- Intensifiers (01, 05) vs expert posture (06): use enhancers on pages and outbound; diagnose and lead in conversation.
- Create a New Game (10's blue ocean) vs Big Fish Small Pond (03): use ERRC for divergence but keep a known category.
- Stated WTP (04) vs behavioural evidence (09): ask the WTP questions, then require a deposit before building.
