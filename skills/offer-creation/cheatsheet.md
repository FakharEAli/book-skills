# Cheatsheet: offer-creation decision rules

## Decision rules (When X → do Y, because Z)
- Starting a new offer → write the job (progress + circumstance) and the competitive alternatives before any deliverable (08, 03), because features chosen first become the offer by default.
- Prospect asks for a proposal on the first call → propose a paid diagnostic with a fee credited against the build (06), because free scoping is free consulting and is discounted.
- Tempted to discount → add a named bonus or remove scope (01, 04), because a discount resets every future anchor.
- Deal stalls after interest → ask "what would need to be true for you to feel safe doing this?" and change the offer's structure (shadow mode, guarantee, pilot) (07), because stalls are anxiety or habit, not missing benefits.
- Prospect says "we'll hire a VA" → compare on 24/7, consistency, monitoring and total cost; concede flexibility and judgement (03, 10), because honest trade-offs beat denial.
- Prospect says "we'll do it in Zapier" → ask what happened the last time it broke and who noticed (07 push; 03 unique attribute: monitoring, reasoning over messy input), because DIY's weakness is silent failure.
- Prospect says "we're fine for now" → mirror the struggling moment and quantify the cost of now; if there is no struggle, qualify out (07, 06), because without push no offer closes.
- Offer page reads like every AI agency → run ERRC: eliminate hourly billing and open scoping, raise speed and ownership, create a guarantee (10).
- Unsure what to charge → ask acceptable / expensive / prohibitive in discovery and build the outside-in case (04); never price from hours.
- Writing any headline or opener → answer the Big Four in one sentence; three-second test (05).
- About to build the system → stop unless at least one qualified owner has paid a deposit or pilot fee (09), because interviews are not evidence.
- Offer launched, no buyers → classify: Feature Shock / Minivation / Hidden Gem / Undead (04), then cut / raise / spin out / kill.
- Choosing a guarantee → conditional, on a metric you control (reply time, go-live date, hours removed), with a short unconditional window (01).
- Picking the first industry → score pain, purchasing power, reachability, growth; require an existing budget line for the problem (01).

## Decision trees
**Which positioning frame?**
- Can you beat the category leader on its own criteria? → Head-to-Head (rare for a small agency).
- Is there a sub-segment the leaders underserve and you can name? → Big Fish, Small Pond (default).
- Neither, and a strong trend you can ride for years? → Create a New Game (expensive; avoid unless funded).

**Which force is blocking the deal?**
- They cannot describe a cost of the status quo → weak Push → quantify or qualify out.
- They cannot picture the better day → weak Pull → demo on their own data.
- They ask "what if it…" questions → Anxiety → structure: approval mode, guarantee, reference.
- They say "the team already does it their way" → Habit → fit into existing channels, parallel run.

**Which test next?**
- No interviews yet → 6–8 timeline interviews.
- Language sharp, no traffic → landing page with Touchstone and three prices; count fit calls.
- Fit calls happening, no money → mock sale with refundable deposit.
- Deposits in → paid concierge pilot, 14 days.
- Pilot renewed → build.

**Where does a feature go?**
- Buyers would buy it alone → Leader: core offer.
- Nice, small cost → Filler: differentiates a higher tier.
- Some buyers actively dislike it → Killer: optional add-on or cut.

## Trade-off matrix: positioning against the alternatives
| Factor | Hire a VA | DIY Zapier | Do nothing | Your offer should |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 24/7 coverage | No | Yes if built | No | Raise |
| Judgement on messy input | Yes | No | Owner only | Raise (agent + human handoff) |
| Silent failure risk | Low | High | n/a | Create: monitoring + alerts |
| Owner time | Managing | Building, fixing | All of it | Raise: zero owner time |
| Flexibility | High | Medium | High | Concede; scope one job well |
| Cost | Monthly salary | Low cash, high time | Hidden | Price in the corridor below a VA's cost |

## Thresholds and defaults the authors commit to
- Three options on every proposal; the one you want sold goes in the middle (04, 06).
- Three seconds for the opener to answer the Big Four (05).
- Price questions by the second conversation at the latest (04, 06).
- Pre-commit the success count before any experiment; counts, not percentages, for small samples (09).
- Build only after real money or signed commitment (09).
- Two to four value themes, not a feature list (03).
- Three or four factors far above the industry, several far below (10).
- Exhaustive problem list before solutions; if it fits in under ten lines, it is not exhaustive (01).

## Tells and smells
- Your headline contains "AI-powered" or a tool name → you have positioned by trend or by stack, not by value (03).
- Deliverables list has no line to a ranked pain → you are selling what you can build (02).
- Prospect asks "how much per hour?" → you have been framed as a vendor; re-anchor on outcome and options (06).
- You wrote the proposal before the money conversation → expect silence (06, 04).
- "Everyone loved it" in interviews but no deposits → desirability unproven (09).
- Offer sells easily to everyone → Minivation; raise price and ambition (04).
- Every client wants something different → segment undefined or scope not trimmed (01, 02).
- Client bought but staff are not using it → little hires failing; fix onboarding before adding features (07, 08).
- Your value curve is the competitors' plus 10% → red ocean (10).
