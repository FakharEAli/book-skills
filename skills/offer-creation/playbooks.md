# Playbooks

Each playbook names the book and framework behind every step. Read the referenced book file when a step is unclear.

## 1. Design one offer for one industry (03 → 02 → 07 → 01 → 04 → 09)

**Goal**: one named, priced, guaranteed AI-automation offer for one business-owner segment, with evidence it will sell before you build it.
**Inputs**: a candidate industry; 6–8 owners you can reach for interviews; your current capabilities list (kept in a drawer until step 5).

**Steps**
1. **Choose the pond** (03 Big Fish Small Pond; 01 Starving Crowd) — score 2–3 candidate industries on pain, purchasing power, reachability, growth; require an existing budget line for the problem (a VA, admin salary, lost leads). Pick one sub-segment you can name ("multi-location clinics", "10–50 person home-services firms").
2. **List the true competitive alternatives** (03 step 4) — for that segment: hire/keep a VA, DIY Zapier or Make, do nothing, the incumbent SaaS's built-in automation. Write each one's genuine strengths.
3. **Fill the Customer Profile** (02) — draft jobs, pains, gains from what you already know; mark every item "hypothesis" until step 4 confirms it.
4. **Run timeline interviews** (07 timeline interview; 08 sources of jobs) — 6–8 owners who recently hired staff for the problem, tried a tool, or fired one. Capture struggling moments, pushes, pulls, anxieties, habits, trade-offs, in their words. Look for workarounds and non-consumers. Write the job as "Help me [progress] when [circumstance]" and a one-page job spec (08). Re-rank the Customer Profile from the interviews.
5. **Complete the Value Map** (02) — now open the capabilities drawer: map products to pain relievers and gain creators; cut anything without a line to a top-ranked item.
6. **Build the Grand Slam Offer** (01) — exhaustive problem list → solutions → delivery vehicles → trim → stack. Choose a conditional guarantee on a metric you control; decide honest scarcity (slots per month); plan one bonus that answers the biggest anxiety from step 4. Name it with MAGIC. Check divergence with a quick ERRC pass (10): eliminate hourly billing and open scoping, raise speed-to-live and owner control.
7. **Run the WTP talk and set three prices** (04) — in the next three owner conversations ask acceptable / expensive / prohibitive; build the outside-in case; sort features into leaders, fillers, killers; set Essential / Complete / Fully Managed with the target in the middle, priced in the corridor below a VA's cost (10).
8. **Write the Touchstone** (05) — one sentence passing the Big Four and the three-second test; it becomes the landing-page hero.
9. **Test before building** (09) — landing page with Touchstone and prices → fit calls → mock sale with deposit → paid concierge pilot for the first two → build. Pre-commit counts on a Test Card for each step.

**Output artifact: One-Offer Sheet**
```
Segment (traits that predict caring):
Job (progress + circumstance):
Competitive alternatives and their strengths:
Unique attributes → value themes → proof:
Offer name (MAGIC):
Touchstone (one sentence):
Deliverables (each linked to a ranked pain/gain):
Bonus (anxiety it answers):
Guarantee (metric, threshold, remedy):
Scarcity / urgency (real reason):
Prices: Essential / Complete / Fully Managed (value metric):
Outside-in value case:
Test plan: experiment → criterion → result → decision
```
**Common failure**: opening the capabilities drawer at step 1. The offer becomes "what n8n can do for dentists" and every later step rationalises it.

## 2. Position against "hire a VA / use Zapier / do nothing"

**Goal**: a positioning document and talk-track that make the real alternatives look like the riskier choice without lying about their strengths.
**Inputs**: the segment and job from Playbook 1; two or three real objections heard on calls.

**Steps**
1. **Name the alternative's strengths first** (03) — VA: judgement, flexibility; Zapier: cheap, in their control; do nothing: zero risk, familiar.
2. **Isolate unique attributes the alternative lacks** (03 step 5) — 24/7, reasoning over messy input, monitoring with alerts, human handoff, zero owner time, client ownership of the build, fixed timeline.
3. **Translate to value themes with proof** (03 step 6; 05 believability) — "Never miss a lead after hours" (reply-time log), "Nothing breaks silently" (alert screenshot), "Zero owner time" (hours removed).
4. **Draw the canvas** (10) — your curve vs VA vs DIY vs agencies; confirm focus and divergence.
5. **Diagnose the force behind the objection** (07 Four Forces) — "we'll hire a VA" is often Habit (people feel safe); "Zapier" is often Anxiety about vendor dependence; "fine for now" is weak Push.
6. **Answer with structure, not argument** (07, 01) — Habit: fit into their channels, parallel run. Anxiety: client owns the build, approval mode, conditional guarantee. Weak Push: quantify the cost of now, then qualify out if there is none.
7. **Write the category line and hero** (03 step 10; 05 Touchstone) — "[Outcome] systems for [segment]", then the Touchstone.

**Output artifact: Positioning one-pager**
```
Competitive alternatives (with strengths):
Unique attributes:
Value themes + proof (2–4):
Best-fit customer traits:
Market category line:
Trend (why now, one line):
Objection → force → structural answer (table, 3 rows)
```
**Common failure**: listing other AI agencies as the competition. The buyer was never choosing between agencies; they were choosing between you and the status quo.

## 3. Price the offer and run the WTP talk

**Goal**: three priced options grounded in buyer economics, delivered with the expert's posture.
**Inputs**: a discovery call booked; the outside-in variables you need (volume, conversion, value per unit, hours, loaded cost).

**Steps**
1. **Decline free scoping; propose the paid diagnostic** (06 Proclamations 3, 8) — if the prospect asks for a proposal first, offer the one-week diagnostic with a fee credited against the build.
2. **Raise money early** (06 Proclamation 9) — "What is this costing you now? What would solving it be worth? Is there a range you have in mind?"
3. **Ask the WTP questions** (04) — acceptable / expensive / prohibitive for the outcome described; purchase probability at your intended middle price; most-least on the candidate features.
4. **Build the outside-in case** (04) — in the buyer's numbers; write it as one equation on the proposal.
5. **Sort features into leaders, fillers, killers** (04) — killers become optional add-ons.
6. **Set good-better-best** (04, 06) — value metric the buyer counts; target option in the middle; top option as anchor; price corridor between the cheaper and the expensive alternative (10).
7. **Protect price integrity** (04, 01) — if pushed: offer the lower tier or add a bonus; never a cheaper version of the same tier; never hourly.

**Output artifact: Pricing block for the proposal**
```
Value case: [volume] × [conversion] × [unit value] = [monthly value at stake]
Option A Essential: scope | build fee | monthly | guarantee
Option B Complete (recommended): scope | build fee | monthly | guarantee | bonus
Option C Fully Managed: scope | build fee | monthly | guarantee | bonuses
Add-ons (killers made optional):
Terms: slots available, start date, rate validity (real reason)
```
**Common failure**: naming a price before the value case is on the table, or sending the proposal cold instead of walking through it on a call (06 Proclamation 5).

## 4. Write the offer page and proposal

**Goal**: a landing page and proposal that pass the Big Four in three seconds and carry proof next to every claim.
**Inputs**: One-Offer Sheet, Positioning one-pager, Pricing block.

**Steps**
1. **Hero = Touchstone** (05) — what, for whom, promise, guarantee or price frame, one sentence.
2. **Believability block directly under it** (05; 03 proof) — demo on a real form, a metric from a live client, the guarantee restated, who you are in one line.
3. **Struggling moment section** (07) — the buyer's own words from interviews; no features yet.
4. **What you get** (01 stack; 02 relievers) — each deliverable paired with the pain it removes; bonus named and valued.
5. **Why not a VA / Zapier / nothing** (03, 10) — honest table from Playbook 2.
6. **Pricing** (04) — three options, value case above them.
7. **Guarantee, scarcity, next step** (01) — conditional guarantee in plain words; real slot count; one call to action.
8. **Proposal variant** (06) — page one: Touchstone, value case, options, guarantee; page two: scope; delivered on a call.
9. **Three-second test** (05) — read the hero to someone outside the industry; they must repeat what, who, why.

**Output artifact**: page skeleton in the order above, each section ≤ 60 words except deliverables.
**Common failure**: opening with the stack or the story of the agency, so the buyer leaves before reaching the offer.

## 5. Test willingness to pay before building

**Goal**: a build decision backed by real commitment, not enthusiasm.
**Inputs**: Touchstone, three prices, a list of 30+ qualified owners.

**Steps**
1. **Write D/F/V hypotheses and rank by risk** (09) — desirability and viability first.
2. **Discovery: interviews** (07, 09) — Test Card criterion as a count (e.g. 6 of 8 describe the struggling moment unprompted).
3. **Discovery: landing page + outreach** (09, 05) — criterion: N fit calls booked from M visitors or messages.
4. **Validation: mock sale** (09, 04) — present options on the fit call; ask for a refundable deposit to hold a slot; criterion: N of 10 pay.
5. **Validation: paid concierge pilot** (09, 06) — 14 days, semi-manual delivery, full monthly fee; criterion: metric achieved and pilot converts to build.
6. **Learning Card after each step** (02, 09) — observation, insight, decision: persevere, pivot a tier, or kill.
7. **Build only after step 5** (09 evidence ladder).

**Output artifact: Experiment log**
```
# | Hypothesis (D/F/V) | Experiment | Metric | Criterion (count) | Result | Decision
```
**Common failure**: skipping from interviews to building because the interviews were warm; or running the mock sale before the Touchstone and prices are sharp, producing a "no" you cannot read.

## 6. Diagnose a flopped offer

**Goal**: name the failure and pick the one fix.
**Inputs**: the offer, its sales record, three lost-deal conversations.

**Steps**
1. **Classify** (04 four failures) — many features and confused buyers: Feature Shock. Sells easily but cheaply: Minivation. Buyers praise a side element you never priced: Hidden Gem. Nobody wants it: Undead.
2. **Re-run the forces on lost deals** (07) — which force was missing or in the way.
3. **Check positioning** (03) — were buyers comparing you to the alternatives you expected?
4. **Check fit** (02) — do deliverables still link to top-ranked pains, or did scope grow?
5. **Decide** — Feature Shock: cut to leaders (04). Minivation: raise price and ambition, add guarantee (01). Hidden Gem: spin the element into its own MAGIC-named offer (01). Undead: kill, return to Playbook 1 step 1 (09 Learning Card).

**Output artifact**: one-paragraph Learning Card with the classification and the single next experiment.
**Common failure**: responding to a flop with a discount, which converts a Minivation into a smaller Minivation.
