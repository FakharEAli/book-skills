# Glossary

Term → one-line definition → (book ##)

- **Anti-guarantee** → "all sales final" stated with a reason, used when a refund is impossible or would attract the wrong buyers → (01)
- **Anxiety (force)** → the buyer's fear of what could go wrong with the new solution; reduced by structure, not reassurance → (07)
- **Believability** → the proof component of an irresistible offer: demonstration, factual, social, credibility, guarantee → (05)
- **Best-fit customer** → the buyer characteristics that predict caring a lot about your value themes → (03)
- **Big Four Questions** → what are you selling, how much, why should I believe you, what's in it for me → (05)
- **Big Fish, Small Pond** → positioning style: dominate a sub-segment of an existing category → (03)
- **Big Hire / Little Hire** → the purchase vs each moment of use; products are fired when little hires fail → (07, 08)
- **Blue ocean** → market space created by redefining the factors of competition, as opposed to a red ocean of head-on rivalry → (10)
- **Competitive alternatives** → what the buyer would do if you did not exist, including nothing → (03)
- **Concierge** → validation experiment delivering the outcome manually to paying customers before automating → (09)
- **Conditional guarantee** → refund or keep-working promise that applies if the buyer meets stated conditions → (01)
- **Create a New Game** → positioning style: define a new category and teach its criteria → (03)
- **Customer Profile** → the jobs, pains and gains side of the Value Proposition Canvas → (02)
- **Delivery vehicle** → the form a solution takes: one-to-one / one-to-many, DIY / done-with-you / done-for-you, support level, medium, speed → (01)
- **Demand-side selling** → starting from the buyer's struggle and progress rather than the product's features → (07)
- **Desirability / Feasibility / Viability** → the three hypothesis types: want it, can deliver it, can profit from it → (09)
- **Dream outcome** → the result the buyer wants, stated as status or money; numerator of the Value Equation → (01)
- **Effort & sacrifice** → what the buyer must do or give up; denominator of the Value Equation → (01)
- **ERRC grid** → Eliminate, Reduce, Raise, Create against the industry's factors of competition → (10)
- **Evidence strength** → ranking from what people say to what they do with money → (09)
- **Feature Shock** → monetization failure: too many features, confused buyer, unrecoverable cost → (04)
- **Fit** → when the Value Map addresses the top-ranked jobs, pains, gains; problem-solution, product-market, business-model stages → (02)
- **Four Forces of Progress** → Push, Pull, Anxiety, Habit; progress when Push + Pull > Anxiety + Habit → (07)
- **Gain creators** → how products produce the customer's ranked gains → (02)
- **Good-better-best** → three-tier pricing; buyers anchor on the middle → (04)
- **Grand Slam Offer** → an offer so valuable relative to price that it cannot be compared to competitors → (01)
- **Habit (force)** → the comfort of the present way; the incumbent's advantage → (07)
- **Head-to-Head** → positioning style: win an existing category on its criteria → (03)
- **Hidden Gem** → monetization failure: real value the company fails to recognise or sell → (04)
- **Job to Be Done** → the progress a customer seeks in a circumstance, with functional, social and emotional dimensions → (08)
- **Job spec** → one-page brief: dimensions, circumstance, trade-offs, obstacles, experiences, definition of done → (08)
- **Leaders, fillers, killers** → bundle features that drive WTP, add a little, or lower WTP for the bundle → (04)
- **Learning Card** → post-experiment record: observation, insight, decision → (02, 09)
- **MAGIC** → naming formula: Magnetic reason, Avatar, Goal, Interval, Container → (01)
- **Market category** → the frame of reference that lets buyers assume your value → (03)
- **Minivation** → monetization failure: right idea, timid price and ambition → (04)
- **Mock sale / presale** → validation experiments asking for payment or deposit before the product exists → (09)
- **Noncustomers (three tiers)** → soon-to-be, refusing, unexplored; source of blue-ocean demand → (10)
- **Outside-in business case** → value built from customer economics, not from your cost → (04)
- **Pain relievers** → how products eliminate the customer's ranked pains → (02)
- **Paid diagnostic** → a scoped, paid audit whose recommendation is the build proposal → (06)
- **Perceived likelihood of achievement** → the buyer's belief the outcome will happen; raised by proof and guarantees → (01)
- **Positioning** → the context that makes a buyer understand what you are, for whom, and why it matters → (03)
- **Price corridor of the mass** → the price range that captures most buyers, set between alternatives → (10)
- **Price integrity** → refusing knee-jerk discounts; conceding scope instead → (04)
- **Pull (force)** → the attraction of the better day the new solution promises → (07)
- **Push (force)** → the pain of the current situation; the struggling moment → (07)
- **Starving crowd** → a market with massive pain, purchasing power, reachability and growth → (01)
- **Strategy canvas** → chart of factors of competition vs offering level; shows value curves → (10)
- **Struggling moment** → the event where the buyer decides the current way is no longer tolerable → (07)
- **Test Card** → pre-experiment record: hypothesis, test, metric, success criterion → (02, 09)
- **Three-Second Rule** → the opener must answer the Big Four almost instantly → (05)
- **Time delay** → how long until the buyer sees results; denominator of the Value Equation → (01)
- **Timeline of a buying decision** → first thought, passive looking, active looking, deciding, onboarding, ongoing use → (07)
- **Touchstone** → one memorable sentence that states the offer and answers the Big Four → (05)
- **Twelve Proclamations** → Enns's rules for selling as an expert, from Specialize to Hold Our Heads High → (06)
- **Undead** → monetization failure: a product nobody wanted, kept alive by sunk cost → (04)
- **Unique attributes** → capabilities the competitive alternatives lack → (03)
- **Value Equation** → (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood) ÷ (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice) → (01)
- **Value innovation** → simultaneous differentiation and lower cost → (10)
- **Value Map** → the products, pain relievers, gain creators side of the canvas → (02)
- **Value Proposition Canvas** → Customer Profile ↔ Value Map; fit between them → (02)
- **Value themes** → 2–4 clusters of value that unique attributes enable → (03)
- **Van Westendorp** → price sensitivity survey: too cheap, bargain, expensive, too expensive → (04)
- **Willingness to pay (WTP)** → what the buyer will pay for the outcome; discovered before building → (04)
- **Wizard of Oz** → validation experiment: human delivers behind a product-like front → (09)
