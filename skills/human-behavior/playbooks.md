# Playbooks — step-by-step workflows for Fakhar's real tasks

Each step names the book/framework it draws from. Book files: [books/](books/).

## 1. Yes-to-Implementation Bridge
**Goal**: turn a verbal yes on a discovery or proposal call into a started project within 7 days.
**Inputs**: call notes; the owner's own words for their problem; their calendar constraints; who else touches the process.

**Steps**
1. *On the call, before hanging up* — book the channel: specific day, time, place/medium, and the one artifact you will bring. "Next step is Tuesday 3pm at the shop; I bring the draft message templates; you bring your phone." (02, Channel Factors)
2. *Same call* — ask the construal question: "When you tell [partner/manager] about this, what's the one-line version?" Give them a rider-ready line if they don't have one. (02 Construal; 05 Rider)
3. *Same call* — map status: "Who runs this today?" Name the long-tenured person and plan to give them authorship first. (06 Social pain)
4. *Within 2 hours* — send a one-ask confirmation: the channel, nothing else. No proposal attached. (08 One ask)
5. *If silence before the booked slot* — don't bump. Re-enter the tunnel: their fire in their words + the one thing you'll do this week + "reply yes". (08 Tunneling)
6. *At the slot* — do the smallest live thing possible (connect one inbox, approve one template) so the first behavior happens in your presence. (07 Start tiny; 02 small forces)
7. *After* — send the first "what got handled" message within 24 hours; it is the celebration and the dopamine hit. (07 Celebration; 01 Anticipation)

**Output artifact** — Call-close checklist:
- [ ] Day/time/medium booked
- [ ] Artifact I bring named
- [ ] Owner's one-line version captured
- [ ] Long-tenured stakeholder identified
- [ ] One-ask confirmation sent
- [ ] First live action scheduled within 7 days

**Common failure**: ending with "I'll send the proposal over" (no channel, maximum bandwidth tax) and then interpreting silence as a no.

## 2. Adoption-First Onboarding
**Goal**: the client's team is using the automation without prompting by day 45.
**Inputs**: the current process (observed, not described); the exact cue that starts it today; the tools people already have open; who gains/loses from the change.

**Steps**
1. *Observe the old habit* — watch the process once; identify its cue, steps, and reward. Don't trust the description (introspection illusion). (03)
2. *Golden Behavior* — Focus Map the candidate new behaviors by impact × feasibility × wanted; pick one. (07 Focus Mapping)
3. *Keep the cue, cut the steps* — trigger the automation from the existing cue; deliver output into the tool already open; new human behavior ≤1 step, ≤30 seconds. (03 Context; 07 Ability Chain)
4. *Write the recipe* — "After [anchor], [staff member] will [tiny behavior]"; confirm the anchor is daily and in the same place. (07 Anchor)
5. *Sludge audit* — list every step from signed to live; delete account creation, pre-fill settings, ship recommended defaults. (04 Sludge, Defaults)
6. *Untangle the old habit* — remove its prompt (shortcut, sticky note, template), then add friction; do this on launch day. (07 Untangling; 03 Replace)
7. *Status pass* — meet the person who owned the old process alone; give them template authorship and their name on the weekly report. (06)
8. *Hive launch* — launch to the group at once, with a name and a first-week number target; time it to a natural disruption if one exists. (05 Hive; 03 Discontinuity)
9. *Feedback loop* — automatic daily or weekly "handled: N" message; immediate confirmation after each approval. (04 Give feedback; 03 Reward)
10. *Check-ins at day 3, 14, 45* — at each, ask the Discovery Question: "What is making this hard?"; treat lapses as friction signals. Expand scope only after the tiny version is automatic. (07; 03 repetition horizon)

**Output artifact** — Adoption spec (one page):
| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Existing cue | |
| Old steps → new steps | N → 1 |
| Anchor sentence | "After ___, ___ will ___" |
| Output location (tool already open) | |
| Default settings shipped | |
| Old-habit prompt removed on launch | |
| Status owner + authorship given | |
| Feedback message (cadence, content) | |
| Check-in dates | day 3 / 14 / 45 |

**Common failure**: a training session plus a new dashboard login; week-2 relapse read as "they don't care".

## 3. Selling Into the Tunnel
**Goal**: get a time-starved owner to engage with and buy an offer they keep ignoring.
**Inputs**: evidence of their current fire (call notes, their posts, staff comments, season); the one automation that touches that fire.

**Steps**
1. *Identify the tunnel* — what is urgent for them this week? If you don't know, ask one question, nothing else. (08)
2. *Shrink the offer* — find the piece of your solution that removes the urgent thing; park the rest. (08; 07 Start tiny)
3. *One-ask message* — their fire in their words → the one fix → "reply yes and I start, nothing else needed". No attachments. (08; 02 Channel)
4. *Lower the load* — one number, concrete mapping ("40 calls a day → 0"), short sentences; send mid-morning midweek. (01 PFC load; 04 Understand mappings)
5. *Default-on commitment* — once they say yes, you collect everything from someone else; they approve once. (08 Vigilance → one-off; 04 Defaults)
6. *Deliver slack visibly* — first report states hours or interruptions removed, not features. (08 Slack)
7. *Return from abundance* — present the full proposal only after the first fix has freed time; frame it as "what the system now makes possible". (08)

**Output artifact** — Tunnel message template:
> "[Their fire, their words]. If I set up [one fix] this week, that's [concrete mapping] gone. Reply yes and I start today; nothing else needed from you."

**Common failure**: a "just circling back" email with the 12-page proposal re-attached.

## 4. Team Adoption Without Status Threat
**Goal**: staff who could quietly kill the workflow become its visible owners.
**Inputs**: org chart of who touches the process; tenure; who gets asked questions today; what each person is proud of.

**Steps**
1. *Status-loss map* — for each person: what do they become less needed for if this works? Rank by social pain. (06; 10)
2. *Moral-foundation scan* — listen for Loyalty ("we look after our people"), Authority ("that's my call"), Sanctity ("our customers deserve a human"); plan answers on those foundations. (05)
3. *Authorship before access* — the highest-pain person approves the templates/rules first and is named as curator. (06 Harmonizing)
4. *Status-safe language* — outputs are "prepared for [role]", never "decided by AI"; the system has an internal name, not a vendor name. (10 Norms; 05 Sacred)
5. *State intent first* — in every staff conversation lead with why you're there: "so you spend your time on the 20 that need a human". (06 Mentalizing)
6. *Public credit* — weekly report to the owner names the staff user and their number. (06 Social reward)
7. *Pair-teaching* — the fast adopter teaches the slow one; learning-to-teach engages mentalizing and sticks. (06)
8. *Hive moment* — one shared launch with the first week's target; celebrate the first number together. (05)

**Output artifact** — Stakeholder sheet: name · role · what they lose · foundation words heard · authorship given · credit mechanism.

**Common failure**: pitching "replace the admin" with the admin in the room; three weeks of "haven't had time".

## 5. Prestige-Led Outreach
**Goal**: outreach and content that gets copied and trusted in a specific trade or region.
**Inputs**: target niche; list of who is deferred to in that niche; your own usable results and failures.

**Steps**
1. *Hidden-motive triage* — what do owners in this niche want to be seen as (modern, in-control, caring, successful)? Check against the questions prospects actually ask. (10)
2. *Prestige map* — list 3 people others defer to (speakers, most-followed, longest-standing). (09 Prestige bias)
3. *Win one node* — offer a pilot in exchange for a candid public write-up; their cost (public endorsement) is a CRED for them, their prestige transfers to you. (09 CREDs)
4. *Self-similar proof* — write the case in the prospect's exact category ("2–4 negotiator independent agency"), with the real number and the two things that broke. (09 Self-similarity; 10 costly honesty)
5. *Conformity, honestly* — "now how N agencies in [county] handle X" only if true. (09)
6. *Content that lets them show off* — one repeatable insight the reader can say at the trade dinner. (10 Conversation as display)
7. *Tone calibration* — plain and understated for established buyers; competitor comparison for growth-hungry ones. (10 Counter-signaling)
8. *Outreach line* — lead with the peer's write-up as a gift, not a pitch; one ask: "want the version for your size?" (09; 08 One ask)
9. *Make them Us* — shared enemy or frustration in the first line (per-seat pricing, the booking platform everyone hates). (01 Us/Them)

**Output artifact** — Post skeleton: self-similar hook → real number → named peer quote → honest failure → repeatable insight → single soft ask.

**Common failure**: a logo wall of unrelated big companies and "cutting-edge AI" copy; passes none of the learning biases.
