# Patterns — every technique, deduplicated across the 10 books

Grouped by what you are trying to do. Book numbers in parentheses. Where two books describe the same move under different names, both names are given once.

## A. Diagnose before you act
1. **Situational post-mortem before character verdict** (02, 01, 08) — rewrite "they're flaky" as "what in their week blocked this?" Attribution error is the default; override it deliberately.
2. **Timescale ladder** (01) — brain state → cues/stress → hormones/sleep → history → culture. Use it to separate a bad moment from a bad fit.
3. **B=MAP troubleshooting order: Prompt → Ability → Motivation** (07; Wood's friction-first in 03 is the same priority) — never start with motivation.
4. **Ability Chain audit** (07) — time, money, physical effort, mental effort, routine fit; find the weakest link. Routine fit and mental effort are usually it.
5. **Tunnel check** (08) — what is the owner's urgent problem this week? If your offer isn't inside it, it is invisible, not rejected.
6. **Construal check** (02) — what does saying yes *mean* about them, and to whom will they have to explain it?
7. **Moral foundation diagnosis** (05) — listen for Care, Fairness (proportionality), Loyalty, Authority, Sanctity, Liberty words in objections; answer on the active foundation.
8. **Hidden-motives test** (10) — compare stated reason with the questions they actually ask; the questions reveal the audience they want to impress.
9. **Status-loss map** (06, 10) — who becomes less needed if this works? That person's social pain predicts sabotage.
10. **Causal-opacity check** (09) — before automating away an "irrational" step, ask what it protects.

## B. Convert agreement into action
11. **Channel factor at the close** (02; action prompt in 07) — date, time, place, named owner, pre-filled artifact, booked in the meeting.
12. **One ask per message** (08) — bandwidth-taxed people drop messages with two asks.
13. **Enter-the-tunnel message** (08) — their fire in their words → one thing you can do this week → "reply yes, nothing else needed".
14. **Remove restraining forces first** (02, 03) — effort, embarrassment, dependence, staff fear; only then add driving forces (urgency, discount).
15. **Prompted choice, not mandated choice** (04) — ask for the decision at the moment it's natural, not up front.
16. **Convert vigilance into a one-off decision** (08, 04) — approve once, default-on thereafter.
17. **Rider-ready line** (05, 10) — give the owner one sentence they can repeat to a partner without sounding sold.

## C. Design adoption so the workflow gets used
18. **Keep the existing cue** (03) — trigger the automation from the event that already starts the old behavior.
19. **Put the output where they already look** (03, 04) — inbox, WhatsApp, POS; a new tab or login is a lost adoption.
20. **Tiny first behavior (<30 s)** (07) — one approval tap; expand only after it is automatic.
21. **Anchor → Tiny Behavior → Celebration** (07) — "After I [existing routine], I will [tap approve]"; the confirmation banner is the celebration.
22. **Immediate, visible, slightly variable reward** (03, 01) — a count of what got handled, right away; dopamine tracks anticipation and novelty.
23. **Replace, don't remove** (03) — give the old cue a new response; habits are insensitive to outcome and persist after the reward is gone.
24. **Untangle the old habit: remove prompt → add friction → lower motivation** (07) — delete the shortcut, archive the template.
25. **Sludge audit** (04) — count clicks, forms, logins, decisions from "yes" to "live"; delete or pre-fill.
26. **Defaults = your recommended settings** (04) — ship with what you would configure yourself; opt-out visible.
27. **Expect error** (04) — approvals reversible, missed steps caught by the system.
28. **Time rollout to a disruption** (03) — new hire, new site, new season; habit discontinuity windows.
29. **Repetition horizon ~2 months with check-ins at day 3/14/45** (03) — lapses in week 2 are friction signals, not resistance.
30. **Golden Behavior selection** (07) — Focus Mapping: high impact *and* wanted *and* feasible; a wanted medium-impact behavior beats an unwanted high-impact one.

## D. Handle people, status, and groups
31. **Become Us before you ask** (01, 05) — shared trade, town, enemy ("per-seat SaaS"); out-group gets scrutinized.
32. **State intent before content** (06) — the mentalizing system is already guessing why you're saying this.
33. **Give the long-tenured person authorship** (06) — the "nine-year person" approves the templates before anyone else gets access.
34. **Make the owner look good to their audience** (06, 10) — team, peers, clients; visible value beats invisible ROI.
35. **Protect the sacred** (05) — position automation as removing drudgery *around* the craft, never the craft.
36. **Hive launch** (05) — group kickoff, team name for the system, shared visible first-week numbers.
37. **Status-safe framings for staff** (10, 06) — "prepared for you", not "decided by AI".
38. **Public credit for users** (06) — name the staff member on the weekly report.
39. **Don't name the elephant** (10) — sell on the stated reason, deliver on the hidden one.
40. **Lower cognitive load for stressed buyers** (01, 08) — short, concrete, one number; retry at a lower-stress time.

## E. Build proof, prestige, and content that spreads
41. **Self-similar proof first** (09) — same trade, size, region beats bigger logos.
42. **Win the prestigious node** (09) — the peer others defer to; offer a pilot for a public write-up.
43. **CREDs over claims** (09, 10) — visibly use your own automations, publish real numbers and failures, bear risk (guarantee with teeth).
44. **Honest conformity signal** (09) — "most [trade] businesses we work with start with X", only when true.
45. **Prestige, not dominance** (09) — teach publicly, let others defer; avoid pressure closes.
46. **Content that lets the reader show off** (10) — give them a repeatable insight; conversation is display.
47. **Counter-signal to secure buyers** (10) — plain language and understatement for established owners; drop "cutting-edge".
48. **Talk to the elephant first** (05) — demo on their data before benefits slides; then arm the rider.
49. **Publicity principle** (04) — never use a nudge you couldn't comfortably explain to the client.
50. **Sell slack, not efficiency** (08) — unallocated hours back are the scarce owner's most valued outcome.

## F. Apply to yourself
51. **Design your own outreach as a habit** (03, 07) — fixed cue (after morning coffee), tiny version (one message), immediate reward (tally), stable context.
52. **Build slack into delivery and pricing** (08) — so you don't tunnel on fires and neglect sales.
53. **Beware your own signaling** (10) — impressive technical solutions are your status display; the client often needs the boring one.
54. **Write the situational post-mortem on every lost deal** (02) — before the character one.
