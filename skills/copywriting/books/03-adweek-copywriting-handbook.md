# 03 · The Adweek Copywriting Handbook
**Author(s)**: Joseph Sugarman | **Year**: 2007 (based on his 1980s seminars) | **Rank in category**: 03/10
**Why it's on the list**: slippery slide, seeds of curiosity, the first sentence's only job, psychological triggers, selling the concept

## Core Idea
Copy is an environment you build so that a reader slides from the first word to the order without friction. Every element (headline, subhead, image, caption, first sentence) has exactly one job: get the next element read. Sugarman, who sold electronics by mail order (JS&A, BluBlocker), treats writing as engineering emotional momentum, then justifying the purchase with logic afterwards: people buy on emotion and defend the decision with reasons.

## Frameworks Introduced
**The Slippery Slide** — copy so compelling the reader cannot stop. **How**: (1) the headline's job is to get the subhead read; (2) the subhead's job is to get the first sentence read; (3) the first sentence is short, almost content-free, and exists only to get the second sentence read ("It was a strange call."); (4) sentences stay short and concrete until momentum is established; (5) the offer arrives only once the reader has slid far enough to feel invested. **Why**: readers decide to continue every few seconds; a heavy first sentence loses them. Failure: opening with the company, the product, or a long qualifying sentence.

**Seeds of Curiosity** — short forward-pointing sentences at the end of paragraphs or sections: "But there's more." "Let me explain." "Here's where it gets interesting." **How**: place one at every point where a reader might stop (end of a section, before a scroll, before the price). **Why**: open loops pull readers across natural exit points. Failure: using them with nothing behind them, which trains readers to ignore you.

**Sell the Concept, Not the Product** — find the idea the product represents and sell that. **How**: list what the product literally is, then ask "what is this really?" until you reach a concept the buyer wants to own (a thermostat is "lower bills without thinking"). Headline the concept; introduce the product as its instrument. **Why**: the product has competitors; a well-framed concept has none. Failure: concept so abstract it fails Bly's "complete message" test.

**Psychological Triggers** (Sugarman lists around thirty) — the ones most useful for B2B: involvement/ownership (let them imagine using it), honesty, credibility, proof of value, justify the purchase, authority, satisfaction conviction (a guarantee that exceeds expectations), desire to belong, curiosity, urgency, fear, instant gratification, exclusivity/rarity, simplicity, storytelling, specificity, hope. **How**: after drafting, audit which triggers are present; add the two or three most natural omissions; remove any that read as manipulation. **Why**: each trigger removes one reason not to act.

**Harmonizing / the Buying Environment** — match the reader's mood and the medium's register; make them nod along ("yes, yes, yes") before asking for anything. **How**: open with statements the reader already agrees with; keep layout and tone consistent with where they found you.

## Key Principles
- Make the first sentence tiny and inviting, because its only job is to get the second one read.
- Write freely first, edit ruthlessly after: express the thought in the fewest words that keep the rhythm.
- Sell emotionally, then hand the reader the logic to defend the purchase to a partner or spouse.
- Resolve objections before the reader raises them; raise the awkward one yourself ("You might think this needs IT involvement. It doesn't, and here's why.").
- Give a satisfaction conviction, not a bare guarantee: "if it hasn't saved your team 10 hours in 30 days, we refund and you keep the workflows."
- Use specifics for believability: "saves 11.5 hours" is believed, "saves lots of time" isn't.
- Let the copy incubate: write, leave it a day, edit with fresh eyes.
- Tell a story when the market is sceptical; stories are read where claims are skipped.

## Anti-patterns
- Opening with "At [Agency] we..."; nobody slides down a sentence about you.
- Long first paragraphs that try to say everything.
- Curiosity hooks with no payoff.
- Over-triggering: urgency + scarcity + fear stacked on a $500 workflow reads as a scam.
- Cutting proof to shorten copy; cut adjectives instead.

## Worked Example (applied to Fakhar's business)
LinkedIn post selling an AI intake assistant for law firms, built as a slippery slide.

> A paralegal messaged me at 11:40 pm.
>
> She was still typing up intake notes from a 4 pm consult.
>
> Not because she's slow. Because the firm takes 60 consults a month and every one ends in 25 minutes of forms.
>
> Here's what we changed. (seed)
>
> The consult is recorded with consent. An assistant we built turns it into the firm's intake form, flags conflicts, and drafts the engagement letter. The paralegal reviews for three minutes and clicks approve.
>
> 60 consults × 22 minutes saved = 22 hours a month. Her words: "I got my evenings back."
>
> The part the partners cared about was different. (seed)
>
> Engagement letters now go out the same day instead of 3 days later. Signed-rate went up. That is revenue, not admin.
>
> If your firm runs on consults and your staff types them up by hand, reply "intake" and I'll show you the three-minute version on one of your real calls.

Trigger audit: storytelling, specificity, involvement (one of your real calls), proof of value, justify the purchase (revenue), simplicity. Missing on purpose: urgency and scarcity, because the audience is sceptical and the offer is a demo.

## Caveats
- Sugarman's examples are 1970s–80s consumer electronics in print; the "buying environment" chapter is about magazine layout and must be re-read as page design and feed context.
- The trigger list is practitioner observation, not experimental psychology; use as an audit checklist, not as evidence.
- He wrote long copy for curious consumers; business owners skim, so apply the slide to the first screen and to each section opener, not only to the whole page.
