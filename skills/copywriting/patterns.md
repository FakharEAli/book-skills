# Patterns: every technique, deduplicated across the ten books

Grouped by the stage of work. Each pattern names its sources by book number; where several books teach the same thing it appears once.

## A. Research and market selection (do before writing)
- **Qualify the crowd before the copy** (09, 01, 04) — confirm the market already wants the outcome, has recently spent on related things, and is reachable. If you must educate them into wanting it, change markets.
- **Mine voice-of-customer verbatim** (08, 05) — transcripts, reviews (yours and competitors', 1★ and 5★ first), forums, support tickets. Copy phrases exactly; tag pain / outcome / objection / trigger / aha; rank by frequency × intensity.
- **Diagnose awareness and sophistication from the mined language** (01, 08) — if phrases name the problem but no solution → Problem Aware; if they compare tools → Product Aware; if they say "I've heard that before" → sophistication stage 3+.
- **Write the positioning sentence** (05) — "[Offer] is the [category] for [who] that [does what]"; check: empty slot, true, wanted.
- **Write the Rule of One sheet** (06) — one idea, one emotion, one story, one benefit, one response. Cut anything that adds a second.
- **Build the BFD lists** (02) — what the reader believes, feels, desires about AI, agencies, their staff. Never contradict a belief in the headline.
- **Collect specifics** (04, 05, 03) — numbers, timeframes, names from pilots or past builds. No specifics → run a sample or pilot first.

## B. Choosing the opening (strategy)
- **Awareness → lead type** (01, 06) — Most Aware: Offer; Product Aware: Promise (claim + proof); Solution Aware: Problem-Solution or Promise; Problem Aware: Big Secret or Proclamation; Unaware: Story. More aware = more direct.
- **Sophistication → claim style** (01) — stage 1 state it; 2 enlarge it; 3 new mechanism; 4 elaborate the mechanism; 5 identification. AI automation to SMBs ≈ stage 3–4: lead with your mechanism, not "AI".
- **Channel desire, don't create it** (01) — the headline is the point where an existing desire meets your offer.
- **Sell the concept, not the product** (03) — headline the idea the buyer wants to own; name the tool later.
- **Headline selects the reader** (04, 05, 07) — name the audience or their situation; let the rest scroll past.
- **Three appeals, ranked** (07) — self-interest first, news second, curiosity only as a booster on top of self-interest.
- **Promise in the headline** (05, 02) — the headline carries a complete selling message because most people read nothing else.
- **Four-function score** (02) — attention, selection, complete message, pull-through; rewrite anything under 3/4.
- **Formula generation** (07, 02) — run the core benefit through 15 formulas (How to / Who else / New / Why / Which / To the owner who / If you / Because / question / testimonial) to break the first-idea habit; pick by appeal and awareness.

## C. Structure of the piece
- **Motivating sequence / AIDA with explicit proof** (02, 09) — attention → need → position the solution → prove → ask once.
- **Order sections by what the reader must believe next** (08) — belief hierarchy, not your org chart of features.
- **Features → benefits → "so what?" chain / means-end chain** (02, 10) — keep asking until the answer is money, time, status or safety; anchor to a Life-Force 8 drive (fear, superiority, protection, approval).
- **Gradualization** (01) — start from facts the sceptic already accepts and step to the claim.
- **Mechanization** (01) — explain how it works to earn belief in a sophisticated market.
- **Inoculation / raise the objection first** (10, 03) — "You may have tried X and it failed because Y; this differs in Z."
- **Two-sided message for sceptics** (10) — admit a real limit to buy credibility for the rest.
- **Tell the full story where the decision happens** (04, 05, 02) — long copy on the page or proposal; short copy where the action is trivial.
- **Satisfaction conviction** (03) — a guarantee stronger than expected, stated plainly, instead of hedged terms.
- **One action, stated once, made easy** (02, 09, 04) — "Reply 'go'", "send me three CVs", "book the 20-minute demo on your own data".
- **Sample before sale** (04, 09) — the first ask is a free, genuinely useful demonstration on their data.

## D. Making it read (flow and style)
- **Slippery slide** (03) — each element exists to get the next read; first sentence short and about them.
- **Seeds of curiosity** (03) — forward hooks at every exit point (section end, before the fold, before price). Always pay them off.
- **A-pile framing** (09) — cold messages look personal: plain text, colleague-style subject, first line about them, no template.
- **Write to one person** (09, 04, 03) — one named reader, conversational register, "you" > "we".
- **Plain words, short sentences, eye relief** (09, 02, 05) — short paragraphs, subheads, white space; read aloud; cut what you wouldn't say.
- **Specific over general, always** (04, 05, 07, 03) — replace adjectives with measurables.
- **Vivid example first, statistic second** (10, 03) — the story is felt, the number is checked.
- **Captions and subheads sell** (05) — skimmers read only these; make each one a benefit.
- **Harmonize with the medium** (03) — match tone and format to where the reader found you (feed vs inbox vs page).
- **Emotion first, logic to justify** (03, 10) — give the reader reasons to defend the purchase to a partner.
- **Incubate, then edit for fewest words** (03) — leave it a day; cut adjectives and throat-clearing, never proof.

## E. Testing and iteration
- **Count, don't argue** (04, 07) — keyed/split tests; one variable; measure the sold action (reply, demo, signed), not opens or clicks.
- **Test order** (07) — headline → offer → lead → length → everything else.
- **Verify winners** (07) — one small test is noise; rerun before you rebuild the page.
- **Keep a swipe file and a phrase bank** (09, 08) — winning structures and mined phrases by segment; copy structure, not words.
- **Move the copy when the market moves a stage** (01) — a headline that stops working usually means sophistication rose; change mechanism or go to identification.
- **Treat tactic lists as a test backlog** (10, 07) — guarantees, scarcity, question headlines, bullets, "free": hypotheses, not rules.

## F. Cross-book disagreements worth knowing
- **Length**: Ogilvy, Bly, Hopkins default long; Wiebe says research decides; Halbert and Sugarman say long is fine only once momentum exists. Rule: long where the decision is made, short where attention is earned.
- **Humour and negativity**: Caples and Hopkins say avoid; social feeds reward some of both. Default to their rule on sales pages and cold email; test on LinkedIn.
- **Hype tactics**: Whitman catalogues them; Ogilvy and Hopkins would reject several as brand-damaging. For B2B owners, side with Ogilvy unless a test says otherwise.
- **Curiosity**: Sugarman relies on it (seeds); Caples warns against it alone. Reconciled: curiosity as connective tissue inside copy, self-interest in the headline.
