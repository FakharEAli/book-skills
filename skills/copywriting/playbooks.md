# Playbooks

Each playbook chains several books into a workflow for a task you actually do. Book numbers in brackets.

## 1. Landing page from voice-of-customer
**Goal**: a service landing page whose headline is in the customer's words and whose opening matches how aware they are.
**Inputs**: 4+ sales/discovery call transcripts; 100+ reviews of competitor or substitute tools (1★ and 5★ first); any client data with numbers (hours saved, response times); your pilot results.

**Steps**
1. **Mine verbatim** (08). Pull every phrase about pain, desired outcome, objection, trigger event, aha moment. Copy exactly; no paraphrase. Tag and count.
2. **Rank by frequency × intensity** (08). Shortlist 10 phrases. Mark the emotion words.
3. **Diagnose the market** (01). From the phrases: do they name the problem only (Problem Aware), the result (Solution Aware), or compare tools (Product Aware)? Do they say "we tried X" (sophistication ≥3)? Write the stage down.
4. **Write the one message** (08) as a sentence a customer could have said. Write the positioning sentence (05) and the Rule of One sheet (06): idea, emotion, story, benefit, response.
5. **Pick the lead** (06) from the stage: Solution Aware → Problem-Solution or Promise; Product Aware → Promise + proof; Problem Aware → Secret/Proclamation. If sophistication ≥3, the headline carries a mechanism (01).
6. **Draft 20 headlines** (07, 02) by running the one message through formulas, using mined phrases as raw material. Score each on Bly's four functions; keep those ≥3. Rank by Caples' appeals (self-interest + news first).
7. **Order sections by belief** (08): what must they believe first (it works) → next (it won't annoy my customers / my staff) → next (it fits our stack) → next (it's worth the money vs the status quo) → next (what if it breaks) → ask.
8. **Write each section as a so-what chain** (02, 10): feature → benefit → so what → Life-Force drive (fear, superiority, protection, approval).
9. **Add mechanism and gradualization** (01) in the first body section for sceptics; add inoculation for "we tried AI" (10).
10. **Flow pass** (03): first sentence short and about them; seed of curiosity at each section end; captions and subheads carry benefits (05).
11. **Proof and ask** (04, 02): a specific number or named example per section; one CTA, repeated, phrased as a sample on their data ("see it on one of your real calls").
12. **Validate** (08): five-second test with two prospects ("what is this, who is it for, what would you do?"); then split-test the top two heroes on real traffic, measuring demos booked (04, 07).

**Output artifact**: Research sheet (phrase bank with tags and counts; stage diagnosis; one message; positioning; Rule of One) + page outline (lead type; hero H1/H2; section order with the belief each lands; CTA text) + two hero variants for testing.
**Common failure**: skipping steps 1–3 and brainstorming a hero; the page ends up saying "AI-powered automation for your business", a stage-1 claim nobody believes.

## 2. Cold email that reads like a letter
**Goal**: a cold email that gets opened (A-pile), gets read (one person), and gets a reply (one small ask).
**Inputs**: a list qualified by recent behaviour; one specific observation per prospect; one relevant result from a past build; a sample you can deliver free in under an hour.

**Steps**
1. **Qualify the crowd** (09): only prospects showing recent, relevant behaviour (posted an admin job, bought a tool, complained publicly, grew headcount). Drop the rest; copy cannot fix a cold list.
2. **A-pile the envelope** (09): plain text; subject a colleague would write about their situation ("the coordinator role you posted"); no logo, template or image; sender is a person.
3. **First line about them** (03, 09): the specific observation, under 15 words. No "I hope this finds you well", no agency intro.
4. **Problem-Solution lead** (06): what the role/task really costs them (in their words from 08 if you have them) → what you built for someone like them, with one number (04).
5. **Means-end, one line** (10, 02): the cost or risk comparison that justifies it ("less than a quarter of a salary and doesn't resign").
6. **Sample as the ask** (04, 09): one action, free, useful either way, on their data ("send me three CVs"). Never "book a call" as the first ask to a cold reader.
7. **Edit for fewest words** (03): 90–150 words, short paragraphs, read aloud; cut anything you wouldn't say across a desk (04).
8. **Test** (07, 04): two subject lines to equal halves; metric = replies accepting the sample.

**Output artifact**: Email template with five slots: [observation] [what the task really costs] [what we built + number] [cost comparison] [sample ask]; subject-line pair for the test.
**Common failure**: opening with the agency and asking for a 30-minute call; the reader sorts it to the B-pile in half a second.

## 3. LinkedIn post that earns the second line
**Goal**: a post that stops the scroll with a recognisable moment, slides to a concrete result, and ends in one soft ask.
**Inputs**: one real client story (anonymised) with a number; the Rule of One sheet for the offer it supports.

**Steps**
1. **Pick the lead for a cold feed** (06): Story or Proclamation. Never Offer.
2. **First line is the slide's top** (03): under 10 words, a specific moment or a defensible contrarian claim ("A paralegal messaged me at 11:40 pm." / "The most expensive employee in most trades firms is the voicemail greeting.").
3. **Second line pays and pulls** (03): one concrete detail, then a seed ("Here's what we changed.").
4. **Body in short lines, one idea per line** (09): what the problem really was → what was built (concept, not tool) → the number → the owner's own words (08).
5. **The turn** (03, 10): "The part the partners cared about was different." Then the money/fear/superiority consequence.
6. **One soft ask** (02, 09): reply with a word, or "if this is you, say so"; no links in the body if reach matters.
7. **Audit triggers** (03): story, specificity, proof, involvement present; urgency and scarcity absent.
8. **Keep a swipe file** (09) of your posts that got replies; copy their structure next time.

**Output artifact**: Post skeleton: [moment] / [detail + seed] / [real problem] / [concept built] / [number] / [their words] / [the turn] / [one ask].
**Common failure**: starting with "Excited to share..." or a listicle title; no moment, no slide, no reader.

## 4. Proposal copy that sells the decision
**Goal**: a proposal the owner can forward to a partner that sells the outcome, not the build.
**Inputs**: discovery notes with the prospect's verbatim phrases; scope; price; one comparable result.

**Steps**
1. **Positioning sentence for this client** (05): what this build does and for whom in their business.
2. **Headline the proposal with the promise and a specific** (05, 07): "Your partners' Monday briefs, researched overnight, cited, for less than one analyst-day a month."
3. **Open with their words** (08, 03): the problem exactly as they said it on the call; they nod before they read your scope.
4. **Motivating sequence as the document structure** (02): need → how it works (mechanism, three steps, 01) → proof (comparable result, sample output attached, 04) → investment framed by the means-end chain (10) → one action.
5. **Inoculate the two objections you heard** (10): name them, answer them, in their language.
6. **Satisfaction conviction** (03): one plain sentence on what happens if it doesn't deliver.
7. **Captions sell** (05): every screenshot or diagram gets a benefit caption, since partners skim.
8. **One ask** (02, 09): "Reply 'go' and the first brief lands next Monday."

**Output artifact**: Proposal outline: Promise headline / Their problem in their words / How it works (3 steps) / Proof + sample / Investment vs status quo / Objections answered / Guarantee / One ask.
**Common failure**: a scope list with a price at the end; it reads as a quote for a tool, not a case for a decision.

## 5. Copy audit in 20 minutes
**Goal**: diagnose why a page, email or post isn't converting and know which book to open.
**Inputs**: the copy; traffic source; whatever metrics exist.

**Steps**
1. **List and offer first** (09): is the traffic a starving crowd? is the first ask a sample? If no, fix these before touching words.
2. **Stage check** (01): what awareness does the traffic have? what sophistication? does the headline start where they are? Mismatch → re-pick lead type (06).
3. **Headline score** (02, 07): four functions out of 4; appeal (self-interest? news? curiosity-only?). <3 or curiosity-only → rewrite from the phrase bank (08).
4. **Specificity count** (04, 05): adjectives vs numbers in the first screen. Adjectives win → get a number or run a pilot.
5. **Slide check** (03): is the first sentence about them and short? does each section end with a reason to continue? are hooks paid off?
6. **So-what and belief order** (02, 08): does each section ladder to money/time/status/safety? are sections in the order the reader must believe?
7. **Trigger and tone** (03, 10): proof, authority, guarantee present? urgency/scarcity manufactured? "we" > "you"?
8. **Prescribe one test** (07): change the single biggest suspect (usually headline or offer), split, measure the sold action.

**Output artifact**: Audit card: List/Offer ✔/✘ · Stage match ✔/✘ · Headline score /4 + appeal · Specifics count · Slide ✔/✘ · Belief order ✔/✘ · Triggers present/absent · The one test to run.
**Common failure**: rewriting sentences when the list, offer or awareness mismatch is the real cause.
