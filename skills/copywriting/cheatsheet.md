# Copywriting cheatsheet: decide fast

## Decision rules
- **When you haven't read customer language for this offer → stop and mine it first** (08), because the headline is in there and brainstorming produces agency-speak.
- **When the market must be taught that the problem exists → change market or channel, not copy** (01, 09), because copy channels desire and cannot create it.
- **When competitors all make your claim → lead with mechanism and proof, not a bigger claim** (01), because you are at sophistication stage 3–4 and claims are discounted.
- **When traffic is cold (LinkedIn, cold email) → open indirect or Problem-Solution, never Offer** (06), because the deal means nothing to someone who doesn't yet want the thing.
- **When traffic is warm (retargeting, pricing-page visitors, replies) → open with Offer or Promise** (06), because delay irritates the aware.
- **When you lack a number for a claim → run a pilot or free sample and get one** (04, 05), because generalities are skimmed and invented precision is caught.
- **When the first sentence mentions you or the product → rewrite it about them, under ~8 words** (03, 09).
- **When the ask is large → precede it with a free, useful sample on their data** (04, 09).
- **When a draft has more "we/our" than "you/your" → rewrite** (02, 09).
- **When told to shorten → cut adjectives, repetition and throat-clearing; never cut proof or the objection answers** (05, 03).
- **When a headline candidate scores <3 on attention / selection / complete message / pull-through → discard** (02).
- **When a hook has no payoff within two paragraphs → delete the hook** (03, 06).
- **When adding urgency or scarcity → only if literally true and explainable in one sentence** (03, 10), because B2B owners punish manufactured pressure.
- **When a cold email has a logo, HTML template or image header → strip to plain text** (09): B-pile.
- **When choosing between two headlines you both like → test; don't debate** (04, 07).

## Decision tree: which opening?
| Reader state (01) | Evidence in their language (08) | Lead (06) | Headline starts with |
|---|---|---|---|
| Most Aware | asks price, terms, "how fast can you start" | Offer | price / terms / start date |
| Product Aware | compares you to alternatives, asks "does it really" | Promise + proof | the boldest true benefit, then a number |
| Solution Aware | names the result they want, not you | Problem-Solution or Promise | the result, then your mechanism as the way |
| Problem Aware | describes the pain, no solution vocabulary | Big Secret or Proclamation | the pain intensified, or a contrarian claim |
| Unaware | no felt problem; talks about identity/ambition | Story | a person like them, a moment they recognise |

## Decision tree: claim style by sophistication (01)
- Nobody else makes the claim → say it plainly.
- A few competitors make it → enlarge it (faster, more, specific number).
- Everyone makes it → introduce a mechanism ("trained on your past jobs, not a script").
- Everyone has a mechanism → elaborate yours (easier, more complete, works where others failed) + proof.
- Market disbelieves all of it → identification: who they are, who you are for, no claim up front.

## Trade-off matrix: copy length
| Situation | Length | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Sales page / proposal for a retainer | Long, complete, sectioned | the decision happens here (04, 05, 02) |
| Cold email | 90–150 words, one ask | attention is not yet earned (09) |
| LinkedIn post | first 2 lines decide; body as long as momentum lasts | the slide (03) |
| Retargeting email to Most Aware | short, offer-led | they want the deal (06) |
| Newsletter signup | one sentence | trivial action |

## Thresholds and defaults the authors commit to
- Headline earns most of the result; spend most writing time there; a headline change can swing results several-fold (07, 02).
- About five times as many people read the headline as the body (05, print data; directional for web).
- Self-interest > news > curiosity as appeals; combine the first two (07).
- Test one variable at a time; headline first, then offer, then lead (07, 04).
- Measure the sold action (reply / demo / signed), never opens or clicks (04).
- One CTA per piece (02, 09).
- First sentence of body: short, about them, no product (03).
- Research time ≥ writing time (08, 05).
- Guarantee: stronger than expected, stated in one plain sentence (03).

## Tells and smells
- Page opens "At [Agency], we..." → you are selling the product, not the concept; no slide (03).
- Hero says "AI-powered automation for your business" → stage-1 claim in a stage-4 market; needs mechanism and audience (01).
- Every section starts with a feature → missing the so-what chain (02, 10).
- Copy is all adjectives, no numbers → no research or no pilot (04, 05).
- You can't say who it is NOT for → no positioning (05).
- Draft has three CTAs → fear of commitment, reader does none (02, 09).
- Cold email has your logo and a template → B-pile; it is being binned unopened (09).
- Reader asks "but how?" and the page doesn't answer → sophistication mismatch; add mechanism (01).
- Reader asks "says who?" → missing proof step of the motivating sequence (02).
- Hooks everywhere, payoff nowhere → clickbait; readers have learned to skip you (03, 06).
- Copy sounds like every competitor → you mined your own vocabulary, not the customer's (08).
- Urgency on a $500 workflow → scam signal; remove (10, 03).
- Campaign flopped and you want to rewrite → check the list and offer first; copy is the third suspect (09).
