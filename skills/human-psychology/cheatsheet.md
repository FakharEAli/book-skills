# Cheatsheet: decision rules, trees, thresholds, tells

## 1. Decision rules (When X → do Y, because Z)
- Prospect answers instantly and confidently → ask "which step, how many times" → System 1 impressions dissolve under specifics [01].
- Any number is about to be discussed → state problem cost first → first number anchors everything after [01].
- Prospect quotes a single failure story → ask for the count and what else changed → one case is an anecdote; sequence is not cause [05, 08].
- Prospect says "we tried it, it failed" → attribute to situation (no owner, agency left), ask for the old scope doc → dissonance will reject blame but accept a face-saving situational story; artefacts beat memory [04].
- Prospect is paralysed by past spend → say plainly it is gone either way and ask what the next pound buys → sunk-cost rule [08, 01].
- Prospect's fear is vague → define the failure narrowly (scope, cost cap, exit) → impact bias inflates undefined outcomes [07].
- You are tempted to paint life-after-automation → replace with a measured outcome and a tally → adaptation erases feelings, not numbers [07, 03].
- Owner describes their process fluently → ask them to walk it step by step including who touches it → explanatory-depth gaps are the scope [05].
- You want adoption by staff → change the situation (defaults, who owns, timing) before training people → situations beat dispositions [08].
- Prospect looks or sounds negative → ask for the label → faces and tone are affect, not emotion [06].
- Prospect is visibly depleted → reschedule the decision → body-budget deficit is read as project risk [06].
- Prospect's objection makes you irritated or relieved → pause; you are soldiering → the feeling steers the reasoning [10].
- Prospect uses identity language ("we've always," "people like us") → reframe as a practical question → identities are defended, claims are evaluated [10].
- Sophisticated buyer → give honest odds plus the failure-branch plan → certainty reads as salesmanship [10, 05].
- Deal signed → send a short confirmation with first milestone → post-decision dissonance needs feeding [02].
- Offering a guarantee → put it where it costs you → cheap guarantees trip the cheater detector [09].

## 2. Decision trees

**Where is the prospect on the pyramid of choice? [04]**
- Never tried automation → low dissonance → educate; small first step.
- Tried once, quietly, failed → one step down → situational reframe + artefact request + reversible pilot.
- Tried, failed, announced it publicly to the team → several steps down → never argue; offer a story in which they were right to try and right to stop; new small private experience.
- "Automation is a scam" as identity → bottom → do not sell; stay in contact with peer stories; wait for a situational trigger (competitor, staff loss).

**Which risk frame? (fourfold pattern) [01]**
- Owner is losing money/time visibly now → risk-seeking for a fix → offer the fuller plan with milestones.
- Owner is comfortable, growth is optional → risk-averse → offer the smallest certain win; lead with peer comparison [09].
- Owner has a sure small loss (a known leak) → will pay to avoid it → price against the leak.

**Which persuasion route? [02]**
- Decision > ~£5k, owner evaluating personally → central route → two-sided message, data, inoculation, argue against your interest.
- Content/scroll context (LinkedIn, cold email) → peripheral route → cues: similarity, specificity, frequency numbers, one peer story.

**What is the stated reason for the stall? [08, 06]**
- "Budget" → check behaviour: did they ask for a price, or go quiet after a stakeholder joined? Reasons are confabulated.
- "Timing" → body budget or status quo bias; apply the status quo bias test [10].
- "Need to think" → unresolved dissonance or committee; ask who else must agree and what loss they fear.

## 3. Trade-offs
| Choice | Gain | Cost | Default |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reversible terms throughout | Lowers fear of yes [07] | Lowers post-purchase satisfaction, invites re-evaluation [07] | 30-day window, then committed term |
| Confident certainty in pitch | Wins unsophisticated buyers [05] | Loses sophisticated ones; dissonance crisis at first glitch | Calibrated: "8 of 10, here's the other 2" |
| Heavy incentive for first step | Faster compliance [02] | No internalization; discount becomes the reason | Small voluntary step, no incentive |
| Emotional future-state copy | Peripheral-route pull [02] | Oversold by imagination, adapted away [07] | Measured outcome + how it is tracked |
| Arguing the prospect's belief | Feels like progress | Adds dissonance, strengthens belief [04] | Situational reframe + new experience |

## 4. Thresholds and defaults the authors commit to
- Losses weigh roughly 2× gains [01]. Price risk reversal accordingly.
- Frequencies over percentages, always [01, 09].
- Reversibility window: short and defined; satisfaction rises once the choice is fixed [07].
- Spaced over massed: 3 short sessions over 2 weeks beats 1 long one [03].
- Reinforce continuously in week one, then taper to intermittent [03].
- Confidence and accuracy are weakly correlated; never use one as a proxy for the other [05].
- Social proof needs similarity (industry, size, stack) more than prestige [02].
- Fear appeals need a specific, doable instruction attached or they backfire [02].
- Expert intuition is trustworthy only with regular environments and fast feedback [01]; most owners' process intuitions do not qualify.

## 5. Tells and smells
- Passive voice about the past ("mistakes were made," "it didn't work out") → unresolved self-justification [04].
- "I knew that would fail" → hindsight bias; their foresight is not better than yours [03].
- Fluent process description with no exceptions mentioned → illusion of knowledge; the exceptions are the scope [05].
- Owner answers for the team on how the work is done → illusion of attention; ask the doer [05].
- Prospect compares you on feature count → comparing at purchase, not at use; redirect to daily experience [07].
- "We're not a tech company" / "we've always" → identity defence; reframe practically [10].
- Instant "that's impossible" from the ops lead → confidence, not knowledge; ask for the specific blocker [05].
- Your own relief when a prospect agrees quickly → you skipped the hard question; run the premortem [10, 01].
- Vivid single AI-failure story driving the whole stance → availability heuristic; supply a vivid, specific success of equal weight [03, 01].
- Asymmetric-looking terms get a disproportionate reaction → cheater detector tripped; restructure for visible symmetry [09].
- Strong reaction right after a stressful meeting or late in the day → affect misattributed to your proposal; reschedule [06].
