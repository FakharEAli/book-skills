# Patterns: every technique, deduplicated across the 10 books

Book numbers in brackets. Each pattern: what it is → how to use it on a business-owner prospect.

## A. How prospects judge (fast, coherent, incomplete)
- **Two-system check** [01, 03] — instant confident answers are System 1 impressions. Force System 2 with a concrete, specific question (which step, which tool, how many times).
- **WYSIATI audit** [01, 05] — list what the prospect cannot see (your base rates, their hidden hours, the cost of inaction) and put numbers on it before the pitch.
- **Anchor first** [01, 08] — quantify the cost of the problem (hours × rate × months, or opportunity cost of the owner's time) before any price is mentioned.
- **Frequency, not probability** [01, 09] — "8 of our last 10" is processed correctly; "80%" is not.
- **Base rate before story** [01, 08] — ask "how many times" before accepting "what happened."
- **Regression guard** [01, 08] — an unusually bad quarter improves on its own; fix a baseline before go-live so the automation is neither over-credited nor blamed.
- **Correlation triage** [03, 05, 08] — for any "X happened after Y": how many cases, what else changed, who self-selected. Treat as hypothesis; propose an A/B test.
- **Cognitive ease in materials** [01] — short sentences, repeated key phrase, one idea per page. Ease reads as truth.
- **Substitution watch** [01] — "Will this pay off?" gets answered as "Do I like this person?" Make the easy question work for you, then force the hard one with a written model.

## B. How prospects feel about risk (loss, reference points, forecasts)
- **Find the reference point** [01] — status quo cost, last vendor's price, or the number a competitor quoted. Frame your offer relative to it.
- **Loss framing with remedy** [01, 02] — describe the loss they are already incurring, then immediately the specific first step. Fear without a remedy produces denial.
- **Fourfold pattern read** [01] — bleeding owner = risk-seeking for a fix (offer the bold plan); comfortable owner = risk-averse (offer the small certain win).
- **Shrink the imagined failure** [07] — impact bias inflates the feared outcome; define it narrowly (pilot scope, bounded cost, exit terms).
- **Do not inflate the imagined relief** [07] — adaptation will erase the feeling; sell measurable, visible outcomes and keep a cumulative tally.
- **Presentism correction** [07, 06] — a stressed owner forecasts a stressful project; name their current state and separate it from the future state.
- **Surrogate over imagination** [07] — a current client describing week two is a better forecast than any case study of outcomes. Offer reference calls framed as "what it was like."
- **Reversibility window, then commitment** [07, 02] — reversible first step reduces fear of saying yes; irreversible term afterwards lets the psychological immune system and post-decision dissonance work for retention.
- **Regret asymmetry** [07] — long-run regret is for inaction; make "another year like this" the vivid alternative.
- **Body budget timing** [06] — do not pitch to the depleted; reschedule high-stakes decisions away from end-of-day and post-crisis moments.

## C. How prospects justify the past (dissonance, self-image, attribution)
- **Locate on the pyramid of choice** [04] — one step down ("we tried it") is recoverable with a reframe; bottom ("automation is a scam") needs a new small experience, not an argument.
- **Situational attribution gift** [02, 04, 08] — explain the prior failure by situation (no internal owner, agency left) rather than by the prospect's judgment or the vendor's character. They will accept it because it protects them.
- **Artefacts over memories** [04, 05, 03] — ask for the old scope doc, invoice, or thread; memory is reconstructed to fit the current self-image.
- **Sunk cost as paid learning** [01, 04, 08] — "that spend is gone either way; it bought you knowledge of which process matters."
- **Face-saving exit** [04] — never require the prospect to admit fault; offer an accurate story in which they were reasonable.
- **Passive voice tell** [04] — "it didn't work out" signals unresolved dissonance about their own role; respond with the ownership question for the new project.
- **Small voluntary commitments** [02, 03] — insufficient justification: a small, freely chosen step (a 20-minute process map) produces belief; a large incentive produces "I did it for the discount."
- **Effort justification** [02] — make the prospect do some discovery work; value tracks effort.
- **Let them say the new belief** [02, 04] — internalization requires their words. "What would it take for you to trust automation again?"
- **Argue against your interest** [02] — name what you would not automate. Trustworthiness beats expertise.
- **Inoculate** [02] — raise and refute the objection their ops manager will make, in the proposal, before they hear it.
- **Pratfall after competence** [02] — admit a specific past mistake only once competence is established; it increases liking.

## D. How prospects overestimate themselves (attention, knowledge, confidence)
- **Explanatory depth probe** [05] — "walk me through it step by step, including who touches it." The gaps are the scope and the overconfidence correction.
- **Ask the doer, not the describer** [05, 03] — the owner has not seen what is not on their dashboard; the office manager has.
- **Written confirmation within hours** [05, 03] — both sides will remember the scope differently; send the map and ask for corrections.
- **Confidence ≠ accuracy** [05, 01, 10] — discount the prospect's confident claims and calibrate your own ("works in 8 of 10; here is what happens in the other 2").
- **Loud vs right in committees** [05] — identify the confident voice and the accurate one; evidence to the first, a private estimate from the second.
- **Reasons are confabulated** [08] — stated reasons for a loss ("budget") are hypotheses; code behaviours across deals instead.
- **Status quo bias test** [10] — "If you were starting the company today, would you run it this way?" Converts "whether" into "what first."
- **Ask for their probability** [10] — "What's your gut number that this process survives two more years?" Their number persuades them.

## E. How to run yourself (the scout's kit)
- **Emotional tell** [10] — relief or irritation at evidence means you are soldiering. Pause.
- **Five tests** [10] — double standard, outsider, conformity, selective skeptic, status quo bias; run on your own deal forecasts.
- **Equivalent bet** [10] — write P(close) and P(ships on time) per proposal; score quarterly.
- **Premortem** [01] — "it is six months later and this failed; write why." Do it with the client; it becomes the risk register.
- **Outside view** [01] — quote from your historical distribution, not your plan.
- **Honest odds plus plan** [10] — give the real success rate and the branch for failure; sophisticated buyers trust it more than certainty.
- **Update one belief by one notch** [10] — after each lost deal, change a specific belief a specific amount; write it down.
- **Your affect is about your body** [06] — a "cold" prospect is often your fatigue. Check before acting.
- **Situations before dispositions** [08, 02] — when staff resist or an owner stalls, change defaults, ownership, timing before trying to change people.

## F. How prospects remember and adapt (after the sale)
- **Peak-end design** [01, 07] — the remembered engagement is its best moment and its ending; make the handover the peak.
- **Adaptation counter** [03, 07, 09] — a visible cumulative tally (hours returned since go-live) against a fixed baseline; the feeling fades, the number must not.
- **Reinforcement taper** [03] — celebrate every success in week one, daily tally in week two, weekly after; variable, then sparse, sustains behaviour.
- **Spaced training with retrieval** [03] — three short sessions over two weeks with quick quizzes beats one long handover.
- **Attribution on record** [03, 08] — log what the automation handled vs what humans handled from day one; self-serving bias will reassign credit otherwise.
- **Post-decision reassurance** [02] — a short note after signing confirming the decision and the first milestone reduces buyer's remorse.
- **Relative standing** [09, 03] — peers' results motivate more than absolute ROI; show where the client sits among similar firms.

## G. Emotion handling (constructed, labelled, functional)
- **Ask for the label** [06] — "what's the feeling, if you had to name it?" beats reading the face.
- **Granularity gift** [06] — offer a precise word ("bandwidth worry," not "doubt"); precise labels are actionable.
- **Recategorise with a reason** [06, 03] — nerves as the feeling careful people have before committing; arousal is relabelled by the context you supply.
- **Predict the bumps** [06] — tell them week one feels like more work and week three goes quiet; predicted discomfort is not alarming.
- **Emotion → goal** [09] — fear sets "don't be the fool" (remedy: reversibility, references); anger at the old vendor sets "deter cheaters" (remedy: milestones, pay-on-running).
- **Cheater-detector-safe terms** [09] — visibly symmetrical exchange: what you deliver, by when, what they owe only after.
- **Costly commitment** [09] — a guarantee that costs you is credible; cheap talk is not.
- **Identity-safe framing** [10, 09] — never ask the prospect to stop being who they are; "a receptionist who never goes home," not "stop being old-fashioned."
