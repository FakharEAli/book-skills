## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

### How prospects judge
**System 1 / System 2** (Kahneman) — fast automatic impressions vs slow effortful checking; System 2 mostly rubber-stamps System 1. → Use when a prospect answers instantly and confidently: ask "which step, how many times" to force System 2, because specifics dissolve impressions.
**WYSIATI** (Kahneman) — judgment is built from what is visible, with no sense of what is missing. → Before any pitch, list what the prospect cannot see (your base rates, their hidden hours) and quantify it, because coherence, not completeness, drives their confidence.
**Anchoring** (Kahneman) — the first number sets the range. → State the cost of the problem before any price, because adjustment away from an anchor stops early. Prefer frequencies ("8 of our last 10") over percentages (Kahneman, Pinker) because frequencies are processed correctly.
**Introspection illusion / "telling more than we can know"** (Nisbett) — people confabulate reasons for their behaviour. → Treat stated reasons for a stall ("budget," "timing") as hypotheses; code behaviours across deals instead.
**Correlation triage** (Nisbett; Chabris & Simons; Myers) — "X happened after Y" is sequence, not cause. → Ask how many cases, what else changed, who self-selected; propose an A/B test rather than accept the story.

### How prospects feel about risk
**Prospect theory and loss aversion** (Kahneman) — outcomes judged as gains/losses from a reference point; losses weigh ~2× gains; bleeding owners gamble on fixes, comfortable owners refuse good bets (fourfold pattern). → Find the reference point first; frame your offer as stopping a loss they already feel; offer the bold plan to the bleeding owner and the small certain win to the comfortable one.
**Impact bias and the three shortcomings of imagination** (Gilbert) — people overestimate how bad failure and how good relief will feel; imagination fills in, leaves out, and imports present mood. → Shrink the imagined failure with narrow, defined terms (threshold, window, remedy); do not inflate the relief; sell measured outcomes with a visible tally, because adaptation erases feelings but not numbers.
**Surrogation** (Gilbert) — someone currently in the situation predicts your future feelings better than your imagination. → Offer reference calls framed as "ask what week two felt like," not "what it saved."
**Reversibility then commitment** (Gilbert; Aronson) — reversible terms lower fear of yes but also lower post-purchase satisfaction; irreversible choices get rationalised into contentment. → 30-day pilot, then a committed term, and say why the term exists.
**Body budget and affective realism** (Barrett) — depleted people experience their fatigue as the riskiness of whatever is in front of them. → Never pitch to the depleted; reschedule the decision.

### How prospects justify the past
**Cognitive dissonance and the pyramid of choice** (Tavris & Aronson; Aronson) — each justified step makes the next cheaper and reversal dearer. → Locate the prospect: one quiet failed attempt is recoverable with a reframe; a public "we're done with automation" needs a new small private experience, never an argument, because argument adds dissonance and hardens the belief.
**Situational attribution** (Aronson; Nisbett; Tavris & Aronson) — people blame others' character and their own circumstances. → Explain the last vendor's failure by situation (built it and left, no internal owner); the prospect accepts it because it protects them too.
**Artefacts over memory** (Tavris & Aronson; Chabris & Simons; Myers) — memory is reconstructed to fit the current self-image. → Ask for the old scope doc or invoice; send your own call notes back within hours for correction.
**Sunk cost as paid learning** (Kahneman; Nisbett) — past spend is irrelevant to the next decision but paralysing. → Say plainly it is gone either way and reframe what it bought.
**Insufficient justification and effort justification** (Aronson) — small freely chosen steps produce belief; large incentives produce "I did it for the discount"; effort raises value. → Ask for a 20-minute process map before kickoff; never discount the first step heavily.
**Credibility = expertise × trustworthiness** (Aronson) — arguing against your own interest buys trust that case studies cannot. → Name one thing you would not automate and why; raise and refute the ops manager's objection in the proposal (inoculation).

### How prospects overestimate themselves
**The six everyday illusions** (Chabris & Simons) — attention, memory, confidence, knowledge, cause, potential. → Ask the owner to walk the process step by step including who touches it (the gaps are the scope); ask the doer what never reaches the owner; discount confident claims from both sides and calibrate your own ("works in 8 of 10; here is the other 2").
**Status quo bias test** (Galef) — "if this weren't the default, would you choose it?" → Ask the owner, "If you were starting the company today, would you run it this way?" It converts "whether" into "what first."

### How to run yourself
**Scout vs soldier mindset** (Galef) — accuracy-motivated vs defence-motivated reasoning; the tell is relief or irritation at evidence. → Run the five tests (double standard, outsider, conformity, selective skeptic, status quo bias) on your own deal forecasts; write P(close) per proposal and score quarterly (equivalent bet test).
**Outside view and premortem** (Kahneman) — forecast from your historical distribution, not your plan; imagine the failure and write why. → Do the premortem with the client on the discovery call; it becomes the risk register.
**Emotions as constructed and as goal-setters** (Barrett; Pinker) — a frown is affect, not emotion; ask for the label. Fear sets "don't be the fool" (remedy: reversibility, references); anger at the old vendor sets "deter cheaters" (remedy: milestones, pay-on-running, symmetric terms).
**Peak-end and adaptation** (Kahneman; Gilbert; Myers) — the engagement is remembered by its peak and ending; the gain stops being felt within weeks. → Make the 90-day review the peak; keep a cumulative "hours returned" tile against a fixed baseline.
