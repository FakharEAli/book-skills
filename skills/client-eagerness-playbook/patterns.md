# Patterns: techniques across the ten books, deduplicated

Grouped by what they do in the process. Each pattern names its source book(s) by number; where two books teach the same move it appears once.

## Choosing and narrowing
- **Alternatives, not competitors** (01) — define what the buyer would do instead, including hiring and doing nothing; position against that. Reused in 10's product-aware comparison and 09's honest comparison.
- **Big fish, small pond** (01, 07) — pick a sub-segment of an existing category and own it; Beckwith's "focus" is the services version.
- **One offer at a time** (01, 03, 07) — one segment, one outcome, one package. Second ideas are parked, not bundled.
- **Rank, then cut** (02, 03) — rank pains by severity and gains by relevance; keep three; Hormozi's trim-and-stack is the same cut applied to delivery vehicles.
- **Category in the buyer's budget language** (01) — "admin", "lead handling", "operations", not "AI agents".

## Learning from buyers
- **Interview before you design** (02, 08) — pains and phrases come from 5–8 conversations; everything written beforehand is a hypothesis.
- **Verbatim capture** (08, 04) — record what buyers say word for word during interviews and SPIN calls; tag it; never tidy it.
- **Mine complaints about the alternative** (08, 01) — reviews of booking apps or VA services hold the sharpest problem-aware language and confirm the alternatives list.
- **Listen before you frame** (05, 04) — Maister's Engage–Listen–Frame order and Rackham's Investigating-before-Demonstrating are the same discipline.
- **Ping as you go** (09, 05) — check the buyer's reaction mid-conversation rather than at the end.

## Making the problem expensive in their words
- **SPIN sequence** (04) — Situation (few) → Problem → Implication (most skill here) → Need-payoff (buyer states the value).
- **Implied → explicit need** (04) — a complaint is implied; a stated want is explicit; only explicit needs justify showing capability.
- **Cost of not knowing** (04, 02) — an Implication question about the missing number ("how many enquiries died?") is often the most expensive pain and the easiest to automate a report for.

## Designing the offer
- **Value Equation** (03) — raise Dream Outcome and Perceived Likelihood; cut Time Delay and Effort. Every scope line must move one term.
- **Problems → solutions → vehicles → trim → stack** (03) — the construction sequence; the stack is presented as one price.
- **Conditional guarantee on a controlled metric** (03, 09) — the honest version of risk reversal; doubles as JOLT's "take risk off the table".
- **Bonus = scope you would include anyway, named** (03) — raises perceived value without raising effort.
- **Smallest proving project** (05, 09) — recommend a first phase that proves the number; lowers self-orientation and outcome uncertainty at once.
- **"Not for you if…"** (01, 07, 05) — a visible boundary that signals focus, credibility and low self-orientation.

## Earning belief
- **Trust Equation** (05) — Credibility + Reliability + Intimacy over Self-orientation; behaviours per touchpoint, not attitudes.
- **Small early reliability** (05, 07) — the recap sent when promised and your own response time are the first demo of the service.
- **Scoped "no"** (05) — saying what you will not automate signals credibility faster than credentials.
- **Evidence audit** (06) — every claim tagged with proof type and specific evidence; unevidenced claims downgraded or removed; scarcity only when documented.
- **Prefer proof that lowers self-orientation** (06, 05) — public process, free sample on their data, honest comparison, over logos and badges.
- **Make the invisible visible** (07) — demo on their data, written week-one plan, sample output, reference; one artefact per evidence gap.
- **Design first and last impressions** (07) — enquiry response and final report/handover carry disproportionate weight.
- **Expectations minus delivery** (07) — state limits explicitly in the proposal so delivery exceeds them.

## Moving the sale
- **Advance, not continuation** (04, 09) — every call and every re-engagement proposes one specific next action.
- **Benefit only after explicit need** (04) — features and advantages before a stated need create objections.
- **Diagnose indecision vs status quo** (09) — agreed the problem matters but will not sign = FOMU; pain and urgency make it worse.
- **JOLT** (09) — Judge decisiveness early; Offer one recommendation; Limit exploration with a bounded "everything you need"; Take risk off the table.
- **Honest comparison, including where a rival fits better** (09, 01, 05) — limits exploration and lowers self-orientation in one document.
- **Reciprocity that is real** (06) — a sample report on their data or an audit of their enquiry handling, given before anything is asked.
- **Commitment ladder** (06, 04) — small voluntary steps (scoping session, data access) that make the larger yes consistent.

## Saying it
- **Stage of awareness first** (10, 08) — decide unaware / problem / solution / product before writing; one message, one stage, one reader.
- **Identification before problem before product** (10) — unaware and problem-aware openings never name the product.
- **Result plus mechanism** (10) — solution-aware messages need the "how" or they are a claim the market has heard.
- **Sophistication check** (10) — "AI" is a saturated claim for operators; lead with mechanism or identification.
- **Assemble from the swipe file** (08) — phrases are the copy; your words connect them. Message hierarchy before sentences.
- **Only audited claims in product-aware copy** (10, 06) — the 06 survivors, the 07 artefacts, the 03 guarantee.
- **Channel ↔ stage** (10) — outreach and social for unaware/problem-aware; landing page for solution/product-aware; proposal for product/most-aware.
- **Loop back** (10 → 01) — alternatives named in real replies that differ from stage 01 trigger a re-run of positioning.
