# Playbooks

The master playbook is the skill. The three short playbooks are entry points into it for situations that arrive mid-process. Each stage names the book it draws from; book files are in [books/](books/), fill-in templates in [templates.md](templates.md).

---

## Master playbook: One offer, one industry, end to end

**Goal**: take one AI-automation offer for one industry from "we could build this" to "prospects in that industry ask for it", producing ten artifacts along the way.
**Rule**: one industry, one expensive operational problem, one package. If at any stage you find yourself writing "and also", stop and park it.
**Inputs**: an industry you can reach (existing clients, network, or a channel you can post in), 5–8 buyer interviews you are willing to book, two to six weeks.
**Common failure**: skipping to stage 10 (writing the page) with stages 02 and 08 empty, so the copy is in your words and the offer is a list of capabilities.

### Stage 01 — Positioning statement · Dunford, *Obviously Awesome* · [books/01](books/01-obviously-awesome.md)
- **Inputs**: industry; your delivered projects; three to five conversations with people in the industry about what they do today.
- **Steps**: 1 list competitive alternatives (what they would do instead, including hire and do-nothing); 2 list what you have that those lack; 3 state the value each attribute enables; 4 describe who cares most; 5 choose the category: big fish, small pond unless you have a reason not to.
- **Output**: template §01 filled.
- **Done-check**: a stranger in the segment could name the alternative you replace and the one thing you do that it cannot.

### Stage 02 — Value Proposition Canvas · Osterwalder et al. · [books/02](books/02-value-proposition-design.md)
- **Inputs**: stage 01 best-fit description; 5–8 interviews booked.
- **Steps**: 1 interview using Problem and Implication questions (borrow from stage 04); record verbatim (start the stage 08 swipe file now); 2 fill jobs, pains, gains in their words; 3 rank pains by severity, gains by relevance; 4 draw the Value Map thin: one product/service line per top pain; 5 check fit.
- **Output**: template §02.
- **Done-check**: top-3 pains come from interviews, each has a reliever, and the top pain has a cost in hours or money.

### Stage 03 — Focused package · Hormozi, *$100M Offers* · [books/03](books/03-100m-offers.md)
- **Inputs**: ranked pains and top gain; the yearly cost of the top pain.
- **Steps**: 1 write the one outcome; 2 list problems before/during/after; 3 solutions and delivery vehicles; 4 trim and stack against the Value Equation; 5 price against the cost of the problem; 6 choose a conditional guarantee on a number you control; 7 name it after outcome and segment.
- **Output**: template §03.
- **Done-check**: one outcome, one price, one guarantee; every scope item moves a Value Equation term.

### Stage 04 — Discovery-call outline · Rackham, *SPIN Selling* · [books/04](books/04-spin-selling.md)
- **Inputs**: top-3 pains; the outcome statement; the Advance you want (usually a scoping session).
- **Steps**: 1 two or three Situation questions you cannot research; 2 one Problem question per pain; 3 at least three Implication questions per top pain; 4 two Need-payoff questions that let the buyer say the outcome; 5 one benefit per expected explicit need; 6 the Advance.
- **Output**: template §04.
- **Done-check**: ≥3 Implication and ≥2 Need-payoff questions; a defined Advance.

### Stage 05 — Trust plan · Maister, Green, Galford, *The Trusted Advisor* · [books/05](books/05-the-trusted-advisor.md)
- **Inputs**: the call outline; your proposal format; your delivery process.
- **Steps**: for each of credibility, reliability, intimacy, low self-orientation, write one behaviour at first call, proposal, delivery. Include one scoped "no" (credibility) and one recommendation that costs you something (low self-orientation).
- **Output**: template §05 (12 cells).
- **Done-check**: every cell is an observable behaviour, not an attitude.

### Stage 06 — Evidence audit · Cialdini, *Influence* · [books/06](books/06-influence.md)
- **Inputs**: your website; your current proposal; the trust plan.
- **Steps**: 1 extract every claim; 2 tag proof type and specific evidence; 3 mark keep / downgrade / remove; 4 list the evidence gaps.
- **Output**: template §06.
- **Done-check**: no claim without a tag; every scarcity or urgency line either documented or removed.

### Stage 07 — Evaluability plan · Beckwith, *Selling the Invisible* · [books/07](books/07-selling-the-invisible.md)
- **Inputs**: the evidence gaps from stage 06.
- **Steps**: 1 for each gap, choose an artefact (demo on their data, process page, sample output, reference); 2 write the week-one plan; 3 write the "not for you if…" list; 4 design the first and last impression; 5 measure your own enquiry response time.
- **Output**: template §07.
- **Done-check**: a prospect can see the process, a sample output and a comparable result without talking to you.

### Stage 08 — Swipe file · Wiebe, *Where Stellar Messages Come From* · [books/08](books/08-where-stellar-messages-come-from.md)
- **Inputs**: transcripts or notes from every stage 02 interview and stage 04 call; reviews of the alternatives.
- **Steps**: 1 extract verbatim phrases; 2 tag pain/gain, awareness stage, source, frequency; 3 pull out the fear phrases for stage 09; 4 pick the message hierarchy: one lead message, supporting messages, proof.
- **Output**: template §08.
- **Done-check**: 20+ entries, all verbatim, all tagged.

### Stage 09 — Indecision plan · Dixon, McKenna, *The JOLT Effect* · [books/09](books/09-the-jolt-effect.md)
- **Inputs**: fear phrases; the guarantee; the process page; the honest comparison with alternatives.
- **Steps**: for each of implementation fear, option overload, "let me think about it": 1 diagnose which source of indecision it is; 2 write the recommendation you will make; 3 the option you will remove; 4 the risk you will absorb (guarantee, pilot, exit); 5 the small Advance you will propose.
- **Output**: template §09.
- **Done-check**: each stall has all of recommendation, removed option, absorbed risk.

### Stage 10 — Messages per awareness stage · Schwartz, *Breakthrough Advertising* · [books/10](books/10-breakthrough-advertising.md)
- **Inputs**: swipe file; surviving claims from stage 06; artefacts from 07; guarantee from 03; category from 01.
- **Steps**: 1 write the unaware/problem-aware opening (identification, then problem, no product); 2 the solution-aware opening (result plus mechanism); 3 the product-aware opening (comparison, proof, guarantee); 4 assign each to a channel; 5 test in real conversations and outreach for two weeks; 6 feed replies back into the swipe file and, if the alternatives named differ from stage 01, re-run stage 01.
- **Output**: template §10.
- **Done-check**: three distinct openings; none leads with the product to someone who does not know the problem; each has been used in at least five real conversations.

## Short playbook: Proposal review before sending
**Goal**: catch the four ways a proposal loses an eager buyer. **Inputs**: the draft; the stage 03, 05, 06, 09 artifacts. **Steps**:
1. *Hormozi* — one outcome, one price, one guarantee? Strip menu options and anything that does not move a Value Equation term.
2. *Maister et al.* — read it for self-orientation: urgency lines, upsell language, more about you than about them. Does it contain one scoped "no" and one recommendation that costs you something?
3. *Cialdini* — run the evidence audit on every claim in the draft; downgrade or remove unevidenced lines.
4. *Beckwith* — is the week-one plan in it? A sample output on their data? The "what we will not do" list?
5. *Dixon, McKenna* — does it make a recommendation rather than present options? Does it name the next small step? Does it absorb the risk the buyer voiced on the call (use their phrase from stage 08)?
**Output**: the proposal review checklist in [templates.md](templates.md) §R. **Common failure**: polishing the design and leaving three unevidenced claims and a bronze/silver/gold table in place.

## Short playbook: Prospect went quiet
**Goal**: restart a stalled, qualified prospect without pressure. **Inputs**: call notes; the stage 09 plan. **Steps**:
1. *Dixon, McKenna* — diagnose: did they agree the problem matters (yes → indecision, not status quo)? Which source: options, information, outcome?
2. *Maister et al.* — check your last two messages for self-orientation; if they were chasers, the next one must give something.
3. *Beckwith* — send one artefact that makes the invisible visible: the week-one plan, a sample report on their numbers, or a recorded demo on their channel.
4. *Dixon, McKenna* — make one recommendation and remove one option: "I'd start with the Instagram channel only, two-week pilot, defined exit; I would not do the full build yet."
5. *Rackham* — propose one small Advance, not a decision.
**Output**: one message, under 120 words, containing an artefact, a recommendation and an Advance. **Common failure**: "just checking in" plus a case study; that answers an objection they do not have.

## Short playbook: New industry in 2 weeks
**Goal**: a testable offer and messages for a new segment in ten working days. **Inputs**: a segment you can reach five people in this week. **Steps**:
1. Days 1–2 · *Dunford* — draft positioning from three informal conversations; list alternatives.
2. Days 2–6 · *Osterwalder et al.* + *Rackham* — five interviews using Problem/Implication questions; record verbatim; fill the canvas; start the swipe file (*Wiebe*).
3. Day 7 · *Hormozi* — one package, priced on the top pain's cost; conditional guarantee.
4. Day 8 · *Maister et al.* + *Beckwith* — trust plan cells for first call and proposal; build one demo you can show on a prospect's own channel; write the week-one plan.
5. Day 9 · *Cialdini* — audit the one-page offer; remove every claim you cannot show in this segment yet (you have no results here; say so).
6. Day 10 · *Schwartz* — write only the problem-aware opening (you have no product-aware proof yet) and the solution-aware mechanism line; send to twenty people; book calls.
7. Weeks 3–4 — run the stage 04 outline on every call; grow the swipe file; add the stage 09 plan after the first stall.
**Output**: stages 01–05 artifacts complete, 06–10 in draft. **Common failure**: spending the two weeks building the automation instead of interviewing; the build happens after the first paid pilot.
