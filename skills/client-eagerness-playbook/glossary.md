# Glossary

Term → one-line definition → (book ##). Books are in [books/](books/).

- **Advance** → a specific action agreed at the end of a call that moves the sale forward (scoping booked, data shared), as opposed to a continuation → (04)
- **Anti-guarantee** → "no refunds, because…"; a stance used when the service's value is obvious or non-reversible → (03)
- **Awareness, stages of** → unaware, problem-aware, solution-aware, product-aware, most aware; determines what the opening of a message must do → (10)
- **Best-fit customer** → the characteristics of buyers who care most about your value; the "who" in the positioning statement → (01)
- **Big fish, small pond** → positioning style: own a sub-segment of an existing category rather than compete head to head or create a new category → (01)
- **Competitive alternatives** → what the buyer would do if you did not exist, including hire someone or do nothing → (01)
- **Conditional guarantee** → remedy promised if a stated metric is missed and the client met a stated condition → (03)
- **Continuation** → a call that ends pleasantly with no agreed next action; a failure state → (04)
- **Credibility** → trust-equation term: your words are believable, including about what you do not know → (05)
- **Customer jobs** → what the customer is trying to get done: functional, social, emotional, supporting → (02)
- **Customer Profile** → left side of the Value Proposition Canvas: jobs, pains, gains → (02)
- **Dream Outcome** → numerator term of the Value Equation: the result the buyer actually wants → (03)
- **Effort & Sacrifice** → denominator term of the Value Equation: what the buyer must do or give up → (03)
- **Evaluability** → the degree to which a prospect can inspect a service before buying; raised by demos, process, examples → (07)
- **Evidence audit** → listing every claim on website and proposal and tagging the proof behind it or removing it → (06)
- **Explicit need** → a want the buyer states in words; the only basis for demonstrating capability → (04)
- **Fit** → when pain relievers and gain creators address the highest-ranked pains and gains; problem-solution, product-market, business-model levels → (02)
- **FOMU** → fear of messing up; the personal risk that drives buyer indecision after the problem has been agreed → (09)
- **Gain creators** → how your products and services produce ranked gains → (02)
- **Grand Slam Offer** → an offer so differentiated and valuable it cannot be compared on price → (03)
- **Implication question** → SPIN question about the consequences and cost of a problem → (04)
- **Implied need** → a complaint or dissatisfaction not yet stated as a want → (04)
- **Indecision** → the buyer has agreed to change but cannot commit; distinct from status quo preference → (09)
- **Intimacy** → trust-equation term: the client feels safe telling you the awkward truth → (05)
- **JOLT** → Judge the indecision, Offer a recommendation, Limit the exploration, Take risk off the table → (09)
- **Limit the exploration** → bounding further research with a complete, honest "everything you need to know" → (09)
- **Market category** → the frame of reference that makes your value obvious; chosen, not inherited → (01)
- **Market sophistication** → how many similar claims the market has already heard; dictates direct claim → bigger claim → mechanism → identification → (10)
- **Mass desire** → an existing want in the market that copy channels onto a product; copy cannot create it → (10)
- **Mechanism** → the "how" behind a result; required in solution-aware copy once the claim is familiar → (10)
- **Message hierarchy** → the one lead message, its supporting messages and proof, decided before any copy is written → (08)
- **Need-payoff question** → SPIN question that gets the buyer to state the value of solving the problem → (04)
- **One reader** → writing to a single specific person at a single awareness stage → (08)
- **Pain relievers** → how your products and services reduce ranked pains → (02)
- **Pains** → obstacles, risks and bad outcomes around a customer job, ranked by severity → (02)
- **Perceived Likelihood of Achievement** → numerator term of the Value Equation: how sure the buyer is the outcome will happen → (03)
- **Pinging** → checking the buyer's reaction during the conversation rather than at the end → (09)
- **Positioning** → the context set so a buyer understands what you are, why it matters and who it is for → (01)
- **Principles of influence** → reciprocity, commitment and consistency, social proof, authority, liking, scarcity, unity → (06)
- **Problem question** → SPIN question about difficulties with the current way → (04)
- **Reliability** → trust-equation term: you do what you said, when you said → (05)
- **Self-orientation** → trust-equation denominator: how visibly your attention is on yourself and the sale → (05)
- **Situation question** → SPIN question about facts of the current set-up; ask few → (04)
- **SPIN** → Situation, Problem, Implication, Need-payoff → (04)
- **Status quo** → the buyer prefers no change; the opponent most sales methods are built for, and not the same as indecision → (09)
- **Swipe file** → tagged collection of verbatim customer phrases used to assemble copy → (08)
- **Time Delay** → denominator term of the Value Equation: how long until the outcome → (03)
- **Trim and stack** → remove low-value, high-cost delivery vehicles and present the rest as one bundle at one price → (03)
- **Trust Equation** → Trust = (Credibility + Reliability + Intimacy) ÷ Self-orientation → (05)
- **Unique attributes** → what you have that the competitive alternatives lack → (01)
- **Value Equation** → (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood) ÷ (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice) → (03)
- **Value Map** → right side of the Value Proposition Canvas: products and services, pain relievers, gain creators → (02)
- **Value Proposition Canvas** → Customer Profile against Value Map, checked for fit → (02)
- **Voice of customer (VoC)** → verbatim language harvested from calls, interviews, reviews and communities → (08)
- **Week-one plan** → a one-page, day-by-day account of what happens after signing; makes the service visible → (07)
