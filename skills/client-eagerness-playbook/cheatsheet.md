# Cheatsheet: decision rules for running the sequence

## Where to start
- **New offer or new industry** → stage 01. Do not touch 06–10 until 01–05 have artifacts.
- **Offer exists, sells inconsistently** → first stage whose done-check fails ([SKILL.md](SKILL.md) table). Usually 02 (pains are yours, not theirs) or 08 (copy is in your words).
- **Live prospect, mid-deal** → short playbooks in [playbooks.md](playbooks.md); do not restart the sequence on a live deal.
- **Two good segments** → run the sequence twice, sequentially. Never one statement for two segments (Dunford).

## Decision rules by stage
- **01** If your alternatives list has no "hire a person / do nothing" entry, it is a competitor list, not an alternatives list. Redo from buyer conversations (Dunford).
- **01** Cannot name the best-fit customer in one sentence → segment too broad for interviews; narrow before 02.
- **02** Fewer than five interviews → pains are hypotheses; mark them and keep interviewing. Top pain has no yearly cost in hours or money → wrong pain for a paid offer.
- **02** Interviewees describe the pain differently → two segments; pick one.
- **03** Package promises two outcomes → split; park one. Price lower than the yearly cost of pain 1 → fine; price higher → wrong pain or wrong segment (Hormozi).
- **03** Scope item moves no Value Equation term → delete. Tempted to add scope to close → it raises Effort and Time Delay; remove scope instead.
- **03** Guarantee metric you cannot measure yourself → change the metric, not the guarantee.
- **04** Fewer than three Implication questions per top pain → you do not know the cost; go back to 02 notes (Rackham). Buyer has stated no explicit need → do not demonstrate capability yet.
- **04** Call ended with "keep in touch" → continuation; next call must propose a specific Advance.
- **05** Any trust-plan cell is an attitude ("be honest") → rewrite as an observable action with a time ("recap within one hour").
- **06** Claim with no specific evidence → downgrade to a description of what you do, or remove. Scarcity line you cannot document today → remove today (Cialdini, Maister).
- **07** Gap in 06 with no artefact in 07 → the claim stays removed. Demo is generic → rebuild it on the prospect's own channel before the proposal (Beckwith).
- **08** Under 20 verbatim phrases → do not write stage 10. Phrase has been tidied → restore the original.
- **09** "Let me think about it" after a strong call → indecision, not objection. Respond with recommendation + removed option + absorbed risk + small Advance; never urgency (Dixon, McKenna).
- **09** "Send more information" → ask what would change the decision; if nothing, it is FOMU.
- **10** Message names the product in the first line → only valid for product-aware readers. Claim is "AI" → the market has heard it; lead with mechanism or identification (Schwartz).

## Decision tree: which short playbook
- Prospect has not replied for 7+ days
  - They agreed the problem matters on the call → "Prospect went quiet" (09)
  - They never stated an implication or need → not indecision; book a new discovery call with the 04 outline
- Proposal drafted
  - Any claim unevidenced, any menu, any urgency line → "Proposal review before sending"
- Segment is new and you can reach five buyers this week → "New industry in 2 weeks"
- Segment is new and you cannot reach five buyers → not a segment you can run the sequence on yet; pick another

## Trade-offs the sequence settles
| Tension | This skill's default | Because |
|---|---|---|
| Hormozi urgency/scarcity vs Dixon–McKenna / Maister | No manufactured urgency | Operators are considered buyers; pressure deepens FOMU and divides trust |
| Broad offer (more buyers) vs narrow (Dunford, Beckwith) | Narrow | Narrow claims are believable and evaluable; broad ones are neither |
| Show capability early vs ask first (Rackham) | Ask first | Capability before an explicit need produces objections |
| Lead with "AI" vs lead with mechanism (Schwartz) | Mechanism | Market sophistication for "AI" claims is already high |
| Authority proof vs low-self-orientation proof (Cialdini vs Maister) | Low self-orientation | Scoped "no" and honest comparison convert where logos do not |

## Thresholds the authors commit to (and this skill adopts)
- Interviews before drawing the Value Map: 5–8. Pains kept in scope: 3.
- Situation questions per call: ≤3. Implication questions per top pain: ≥3. Need-payoff: ≥2.
- Trust-plan cells: 12 (4 terms × 3 touchpoints).
- Swipe-file entries before writing copy: 20+. Conversations per message before judging it: 5.
- Guarantee: conditional, on a metric you control; one per package.

## Tells
- Proposal contains "bronze/silver/gold" → you have skipped 03 trimming and created option overload for 09.
- Landing page opens with your product name → it serves only product-aware readers; nobody else gets past line one.
- Your call notes contain no verbatim quotes → stage 08 will be written in your voice.
- You can describe the pain better than the buyer can → you have been pitching, not asking.
- You feel the urge to add a deadline → the deal is stalled on FOMU and you are about to make it worse.
- Last client contact was an invoice → Beckwith's last impression is the invoice; send the final report.
