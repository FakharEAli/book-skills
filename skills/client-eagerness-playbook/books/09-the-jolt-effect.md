# 09 · The JOLT Effect
**Author(s)**: Matthew Dixon, Ted McKenna | **Year**: 2022 | **Rank in category**: 09/10
**Why it's on the list**: By this stage prospects agree the problem is real and the package fits, and some still do not sign. This book explains that stall and what to do instead of pushing.
**Artifact this stage produces**: an indecision plan covering implementation fears, option overload, and "let me think about it".
**Fuller treatment**: sibling skill `sales-psychology` → [`02-the-jolt-effect.md`](../../sales-psychology/books/02-the-jolt-effect.md)

## Core Idea
Analysing recorded sales conversations, Dixon and McKenna found that a large share of lost deals were lost not to the status quo but to customer indecision: buyers who had already agreed to change but could not commit. The cause is **FOMU**, fear of messing up, which is a personal risk to the buyer, not a doubt about the problem. Classic tactics (restating the pain, adding urgency) address status quo and make indecision worse.

## The Framework You Use Here
**Three sources of indecision** (Dixon, McKenna):
- **Valuation problems** — too many options, cannot tell which is right.
- **Lack of information** — feel they have not done enough homework.
- **Outcome uncertainty** — unsure the promised result will materialise, and that they will be blamed if it does not.

**JOLT** (Dixon, McKenna):
- **J — Judge the indecision** — assess early how decisive this buyer is and which of the three sources is active; qualify on ability to decide, not only on need.
- **O — Offer a recommendation** — stop presenting options; say which one and why. A recommendation transfers part of the responsibility from buyer to seller.
- **L — Limit the exploration** — set a boundary on further research: "here is what you need to know; here is what more research will not change". Anticipate the next three questions and answer them unasked.
- **T — Take risk off the table** — downside protection: a guarantee, a smaller first phase, a defined exit, an opt-out point. This addresses outcome uncertainty directly.

Dixon and McKenna also note that high performers use "pinging" (checking the buyer's reaction as they go) and act as the buyer's agent rather than the vendor's advocate; this is [05](05-the-trusted-advisor.md)'s low self-orientation measured in calls.

## Decision Rules for This Stage
- Diagnose before you act. "Let me think about it" after a strong call is FOMU, not objection; sending more case studies answers the wrong question.
- For each stall type, pre-write the move: valuation → one recommendation; lack of information → a bounded "everything you need" page; outcome uncertainty → the stage 03 guarantee or a smaller first phase.
- When a buyer asks to compare you against others, make the comparison for them in writing, including where a competitor is a better fit. Limiting exploration honestly is the fastest route to the decision.
- Never add urgency to a stalled deal. Pressure increases the perceived cost of being wrong.
- Keep the Advance discipline from [04](04-spin-selling.md): a stalled deal needs one small next action, not a decision.

## Anti-patterns
- Status-quo tactics on an indecision stall: "the cost of inaction is…", "prices go up next month".
- Offering more options to show flexibility. Options are the problem.
- Treating "send me more information" as a buying signal and sending everything.

## Worked Example
Indecision plan for the studio offer:
| Stall | Diagnosis | Move |
|---|---|---|
| "Will it sound like a robot to my members?" | Outcome uncertainty / implementation fear | Demo on their own account (stage 07) + handover rule: anything emotional or complaint-like goes to a human. Guarantee covers response time, which is in your control. |
| "We're also looking at two other agencies and a VA" | Valuation problem | One-page honest comparison; recommend the VA if enquiry volume is under 40/month; recommend you above it. |
| "Let me think about it and come back to you" | FOMU, undiagnosed | Ask what would need to be true to decide; propose a 2-week paid pilot on one channel with a defined exit. |
| "Can you send more case studies?" | Lack of information, possibly FOMU | Send one named reference plus the week-one plan; state that further material will not change the answer. |

## Caveats
Proprietary analysis of recorded calls by the authors' firm; the categories are plausible and consistent with [05](05-the-trusted-advisor.md), but the proportions should not be treated as established. The book argues directly against Hormozi's urgency devices in [03](03-100m-offers.md); this skill follows Dixon and McKenna for considered B2B purchases.
