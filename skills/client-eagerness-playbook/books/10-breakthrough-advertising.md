# 10 · Breakthrough Advertising
**Author(s)**: Eugene Schwartz | **Year**: 1966 | **Rank in category**: 10/10

**Artifact this stage produces**: a message per awareness stage: unaware of the problem, exploring solutions, comparing providers.
**Fuller treatment**: sibling skill `copywriting` → [`01-breakthrough-advertising.md`](../../copywriting/books/01-breakthrough-advertising.md)

## Core Idea
Advertising cannot create desire; it can only channel desire that already exists onto a particular product. The copywriter's job is to identify what the reader already wants and already knows, and to meet them exactly there. Two variables determine the opening: how aware the reader is of their problem and your solution, and how sophisticated the market has become about claims like yours.

## The Framework You Use Here
**Five stages of awareness** (Schwartz) — the headline's job changes at each:
1. **Unaware** — does not know they have the problem. Open with identification: the reader's situation or identity, not the problem or the product.
2. **Problem-aware** — feels the pain, does not know solutions exist. Open with the problem in their words; promise it can be solved.
3. **Solution-aware** — knows the kind of solution exists, not your product. Open with the result and the mechanism that makes it credible.
4. **Product-aware** — knows you, is comparing. Open with the specific claim, proof and offer; differentiation against alternatives.
5. **Most aware** — ready; needs only the offer and the terms.

Your three required messages map as: "unaware of problem" → stages 1–2; "exploring solutions" → stage 3; "comparing providers" → stage 4.

**Stages of market sophistication** (Schwartz) — how many similar claims the market has already heard. A new claim can be stated directly; once competitors repeat it, you must enlarge the claim, then introduce a mechanism, then elaborate the mechanism, and finally, when the market is exhausted, lead with identification rather than claims. "AI automation" is already a crowded claim for operators; lead with mechanism and identification, not with "AI".

## Decision Rules for This Stage
- Decide the stage before writing a word. One message, one stage, one reader (stage 08's "one reader").
- Build each message from swipe-file phrases tagged to that stage; your own words only connect them.
- Unaware/problem-aware messages never name the product in the opening. Naming it to someone who does not know the problem reads as a pitch and is skipped.
- Solution-aware messages must contain the mechanism ("an agent that reads the DM and books the trial directly in your calendar"); a result without a mechanism is a claim the market has heard.
- Product-aware messages may only use claims that survived the stage 06 audit, and should include the stage 07 artefacts and the stage 03 guarantee.
- Match channel to stage: LinkedIn posts and cold outreach reach unaware and problem-aware readers; the landing page is mostly solution- and product-aware; the proposal is product-aware to most-aware.

## Anti-patterns
- One landing page that tries to serve all stages, usually by leading with the product and losing everyone before stage 4.
- Leading with "AI" as the claim in a market that has heard it from every agency this year.
- Writing the unaware message as a scare ("you are losing leads"); identification first, problem second.

## Worked Example
Three openings for the studio offer, built from the stage 08 swipe file:
- **Unaware / problem-aware** (LinkedIn post, cold DM): *You run the studio, coach the 6pm class, and answer DMs at 11pm in bed. Most owners I talk to can't say how many enquiries they lost last month. Here's how three of them found out.* No product named.
- **Solution-aware** (landing page hero): *Every Instagram and WhatsApp enquiry answered in under a minute, and the trial booked straight into your calendar, by an agent trained on your offers, while your desk team stays with the members in the room.* Mechanism stated.
- **Product-aware** (comparison page, proposal cover): *Compared with a booking app, it answers instead of waiting to be found. Compared with a VA, it is awake at 11pm and costs less than a part-time shift. Watch it answer a DM on your own account in the scoping call; if median response ever exceeds two minutes in a week, that month is free.* Proof and guarantee.

## Caveats
Written for direct-response print in 1966; the mechanics of channels have changed, the stage logic has not. Schwartz's sophistication stages are an argument from experience, not research. Later authors on this list ([08](08-where-stellar-messages-come-from.md)) operationalise the research he assumes.
