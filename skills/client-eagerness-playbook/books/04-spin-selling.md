# 04 · SPIN Selling
**Author(s)**: Neil Rackham | **Year**: 1988 | **Rank in category**: 04/10
**Why it's on the list**: The package is only worth what the buyer believes the problem costs. This is the method for getting the buyer to state that cost out loud.
**Artifact this stage produces**: a discovery-call outline that uncovers the problem and its business impact.
**Fuller treatment**: sibling skill `sales-psychology` → [`01-spin-selling.md`](../../sales-psychology/books/01-spin-selling.md)

## Core Idea
Rackham's field research on sales calls found that in larger sales, success correlated with the questions the seller asked, not with closing techniques or feature pitches. Buyers who articulate the implications of their own problems, and then articulate the value of solving them, move forward; buyers who are pitched at do not.

## The Framework You Use Here
**SPIN** (Rackham) — four question types, used in roughly this order within the Investigating stage of a call:
- **Situation** — facts about their current set-up. Ask few; buyers find these tedious. Do your research so you need only two or three.
- **Problem** — difficulties and dissatisfactions with the current way. These surface **implied needs** ("the desk can't keep up").
- **Implication** — the consequences of the problem: cost, knock-on effects, who else it hurts. This is where the problem becomes expensive in the buyer's own words. Rackham's research puts most of the skill of top performers here.
- **Need-payoff** — the value or usefulness of a solution, asked so the buyer says it: "if enquiries were answered inside a minute, what would that do for trial bookings?" These convert implied needs into **explicit needs**, and the buyer rehearses your proposal for you.

**Four stages of a call** (Rackham) — Preliminaries (brief), Investigating (most of the call), Demonstrating Capability (benefits tied to explicit needs, not features), Obtaining Commitment.

**Advance vs continuation** (Rackham) — an **Advance** is a specific action that moves the sale on (a scoping session booked, data access granted, the decision-maker introduced). A **continuation** is "sounds great, keep in touch". Plan the Advance you want before the call.

**Features, Advantages, Benefits** (Rackham) — a Benefit shows how you meet an explicit need the buyer has stated. Describing capabilities before the buyer has stated a need produces objections.

## Decision Rules for This Stage
- Write at least three Implication questions per top pain from stage 02. If you cannot, you do not yet understand the cost of the problem.
- Write Need-payoff questions that let the buyer describe the stage 03 outcome in their own words. Record those words; they go to stage 08.
- Do not demonstrate capability until you have at least one explicit need stated by the buyer.
- End every call with a proposed Advance. If the buyer declines it, you have learned something; if you never proposed one, you have learned nothing.

## Anti-patterns
- Opening with a demo. It answers questions the buyer has not asked and invites "how much?" before value exists.
- Stacking Situation questions ("how many staff, which CRM, what hours") to look thorough. Rackham found these correlate with buyer boredom, not success.
- Treating "let me think about it" as a continuation to accept. See [09](09-the-jolt-effect.md).

## Worked Example
Discovery outline for a studio owner (30 minutes):
- Preliminaries (2 min): purpose, agenda, permission to ask questions.
- Situation (3): "Who answers DMs today, and when?" "Roughly how many enquiries a week?"
- Problem (5): "What happens to a DM that comes in during a 6pm class?" "How often does a lead reply after you've answered the next morning?"
- Implication (10): "When a lead goes cold, where do they usually end up?" "What's a member worth to you over a year?" "How does the desk team feel about being on their phone during sessions?" "What does it cost you not to know how many enquiries died last month?"
- Need-payoff (7): "If every enquiry got a reply inside a minute, what would change for trial bookings?" "How useful would a Monday number for lost versus recovered enquiries be?"
- Capability (2): one benefit per explicit need stated.
- Commitment (1): Advance = a 45-minute scoping session with access to their enquiry channels.

## Caveats
Rackham's study is proprietary practitioner research, never independently replicated at scale; treat the pattern as robust practice rather than law. SPIN assumes the buyer will take a call; getting the call is stage 10's job.
