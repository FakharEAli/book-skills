# 05 · The Trusted Advisor
**Author(s)**: David Maister, Charles Green, Robert Galford | **Year**: 2000 | **Rank in category**: 05/10
**Why it's on the list**: An operator buying an invisible, technical service is buying the person. This gives you a plan for being believed, from first call through delivery.
**Artifact this stage produces**: a trust plan: one concrete behaviour each for credibility, reliability, intimacy and low self-orientation, at first call, proposal and delivery.
**Fuller treatment**: sibling skill `sales-psychology` → [`03-the-trusted-advisor.md`](../../sales-psychology/books/03-the-trusted-advisor.md)

## Core Idea
Clients do not hire the most expert adviser; they hire the one they trust, and trust is earned through observable behaviour over time, not asserted through credentials. The authors' claim is that trust is built in small moments where the adviser visibly puts the client's interest ahead of the sale.

## The Framework You Use Here
**Trust Equation** (Maister, Green, Galford) — Trust = (Credibility + Reliability + Intimacy) ÷ Self-orientation.
- **Credibility** — words: do you know what you are talking about, and do you say so honestly, including what you do not know.
- **Reliability** — actions: do you do what you said, when you said.
- **Intimacy** — safety: can the client tell you the awkward truth (the real reason the admin is a mess) without being judged.
- **Self-orientation** — the denominator: how much of your attention is visibly on yourself, your fee, your close. High self-orientation divides everything else.

**Trust-building stages** (Maister, Green, Galford) — Engage (earn the right to talk about the issue), Listen (so the client feels heard; listening precedes advising), Frame (name the problem in a way the client recognises), Envision (what the solved state looks like), Commit (agree what happens next). Advice given before Listen and Frame is rejected regardless of quality.

## Decision Rules for This Stage
- For each of the four terms, write one behaviour you will perform at each of three touchpoints: first call, proposal, delivery. Twelve behaviours; that is the artifact.
- Credibility behaviours should include saying what you will *not* automate and why. Nothing signals credibility faster than a scoped "no".
- Reliability behaviours must be small and early: send the recap within the hour you promised; show up to the scoping call with the thing you said you would bring.
- Intimacy behaviours are mostly about what you do not do: no visible judgement when they show you the spreadsheet chaos.
- Low self-orientation behaviours cost you something visible: recommending a smaller first project; naming a cheaper alternative when it fits.

## Anti-patterns
- Leading with credentials and logos. The authors are clear that credibility is necessary but weakest on its own.
- Advising before listening. Framing the problem before the client has described it reads as a pitch.
- Letting self-orientation leak in proposals: upsell language, urgency, "limited availability".

## Worked Example
Trust plan for the studio offer:
| | First call | Proposal | Delivery |
|---|---|---|---|
| Credibility | Say which enquiry types the agent will hand to a human and why | Include the failure modes and how each is handled | Weekly report shows misses as well as recoveries |
| Reliability | Send the recap and next step within one hour, as promised on the call | Send on the day you said; no silent slips | Go-live date met or re-agreed a week ahead, never the day before |
| Intimacy | Ask about the DM backlog without commenting on it | Reflect their words for the pain (from stage 08) rather than yours | Private channel for "this answer was wrong" with no defensiveness |
| Low self-orientation | Propose a scoping session, not a signature | Recommend the smallest project that proves the number | Tell them when a second project is not worth it yet |

## Caveats
The book is written for consulting and professional-services partners with long engagements; a short automation build compresses the stages, which makes the first-call and proposal behaviours disproportionately important. The Trust Equation is a heuristic, not a measured model.
