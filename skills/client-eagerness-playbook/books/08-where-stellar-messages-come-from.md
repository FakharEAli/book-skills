# 08 · Where Stellar Messages Come From
**Author(s)**: Joanna Wiebe | **Year**: 2023 | **Rank in category**: 08/10
**Why it's on the list**: By now you have had real conversations. Wiebe's argument is that the message is already in them; your job is to collect it, not compose it.
**Artifact this stage produces**: a swipe file of customer phrases from calls and interviews, tagged by pain/gain and by stage of awareness.
**Fuller treatment**: sibling skill `copywriting` → [`08-where-stellar-messages-come-from.md`](../../copywriting/books/08-where-stellar-messages-come-from.md)

## Core Idea
Strong messaging is found, not invented: it comes from what customers already say about the problem, the alternatives, the moment they decided and the result they got. Wiebe's position is that messaging strategy precedes copy, that the strategy is built from voice-of-customer data, and that writing before the research is done produces copy that sounds like the seller rather than the buyer.

## The Framework You Use Here
Wiebe's method, reduced to what this stage needs:
- **Voice-of-customer (VoC) mining** — harvest verbatim language from discovery calls, interviews, support threads, reviews of the alternatives, and community posts in the segment. Record the exact phrase, who said it, and the context.
- **Message hierarchy** — from the mined phrases, decide the one message everything else supports, then the supporting messages, then the proof points. The hierarchy governs the page before any sentence is written.
- **One reader** — write to one specific person in the segment at one stage of awareness, not to a composite.
- **Stage-of-awareness tagging** — Wiebe builds on Schwartz ([10](10-breakthrough-advertising.md)): each phrase is tagged by whether the speaker was unaware, problem-aware, solution-aware or product-aware when they said it, because a phrase only works on readers at the same stage.

The swipe file is therefore not a quotes folder. Each entry carries: the verbatim phrase · source (call, interview, review) · pain or gain it expresses (mapped to stage 02) · awareness stage · how often you have heard something like it.

## Decision Rules for This Stage
- Capture phrases during stage 04 calls and stage 02 interviews as they happen; transcribe, do not paraphrase. Your paraphrase is your voice, which is the thing you are trying to remove.
- Require 20+ entries before writing anything at stage 10. Fewer and you will fill the gaps with your own words.
- Weight frequency over eloquence: a clumsy phrase heard five times beats a vivid one heard once.
- Mine reviews of the *alternatives* (booking apps, VA services) for problem-aware language; buyers describe the pain most sharply when complaining about the thing they use now.
- Tag the Need-payoff answers from stage 04 as gain phrases; they are your outcome wording for stage 03 and 10.

## Anti-patterns
- Writing the landing page first and "validating" it with customer quotes afterwards.
- Cleaning up phrases into marketing language. "I'm answering DMs at 11pm in bed" is the headline; "24/7 responsiveness" is not.
- Mixing stages: a product-aware phrase ("does it integrate with Mindbody?") in an unaware headline.

## Worked Example
Swipe-file entries for the studio offer:
| Phrase (verbatim) | Source | Pain/Gain (stage 02) | Awareness | Heard |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| "By the time I reply they've already joined the place down the road" | Discovery call | Pain 1 | Problem-aware | 4× |
| "I'm answering DMs at 11pm in bed" | Interview | Pain 2 (emotional) | Problem-aware | 3× |
| "I honestly don't know how many we lose" | Interview | Pain 3 | Problem-aware | 5× |
| "If it just booked the trial for me, that's the whole job" | Need-payoff answer | Gain 1 | Solution-aware | 2× |
| "Does it sound like a robot?" | Scoping session | Fear (stage 09) | Product-aware | 3× |
| "The booking app only works if they find the link" | Review of alternative | Pain 1 | Problem-aware | 2× |

## Caveats
This is a recent practitioner book and its method is Copyhackers' practice rather than tested theory; it is on the list because it operationalises the collection step that Schwartz assumes you have done. Wiebe writes mostly for SaaS conversion copy; for a services agency the interview and call sources matter more than review mining.
