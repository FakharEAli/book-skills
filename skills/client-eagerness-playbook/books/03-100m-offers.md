# 03 · $100M Offers
**Author(s)**: Alex Hormozi | **Year**: 2021 | **Rank in category**: 03/10
**Why it's on the list**: Interviews told you what hurts; this turns it into one package a buyer feels stupid saying no to, priced on the cost of the problem rather than on your hours.
**Artifact this stage produces**: one focused package: outcome, scope, price, terms, guarantee.
**Fuller treatment**: sibling skill `offer-creation` → [`01-100m-offers.md`](../../offer-creation/books/01-100m-offers.md)

## Core Idea
Hormozi's thesis is that the offer, not the salesmanship, does most of the work: a "Grand Slam Offer" is so differentiated and so clearly valuable that it cannot be compared on price. You get there by choosing a market with a painful problem and purchasing power, pricing on value, and stacking the solution so that perceived value far exceeds price.

## The Framework You Use Here
**Value Equation** (Hormozi) — perceived value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Achievement) ÷ (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice). Every element of scope must raise the top or shrink the bottom; anything else is cost with no value.

**Offer construction** (Hormozi) — the steps:
1. Identify the dream outcome for the segment (from the stage 02 top gain).
2. List every problem the buyer hits before, during and after getting that outcome.
3. Turn each problem into a solution statement ("how to answer every DM in under a minute without hiring").
4. List delivery vehicles for each solution (done-for-you build, template, training, monitoring, report).
5. **Trim and stack**: keep the vehicles with high value and low cost to you; drop the rest; present what remains as one bundle with a single price.

**Enhancers** (Hormozi) — scarcity, urgency, bonuses, guarantees, naming. Use bonuses (components you would include anyway, named separately) and a guarantee; be sparing with scarcity and urgency, see Caveats. Guarantee types: unconditional, conditional (outcome tied to a condition such as "if you follow the onboarding"), anti-guarantee ("no refunds, because…"), implied (performance-based pricing). For a first project in a new industry, a conditional guarantee tied to a measurable number is usually the honest choice.

**Pricing** (Hormozi) — price on the gap between what the problem costs and what the outcome is worth, not on build hours. He argues that charging more makes clients more committed and lets you deliver better, which raises results and justifies the price; charging less does the reverse.

## Decision Rules for This Stage
- One outcome. If the package promises two outcomes, split it and park one.
- Price against the stage 02 severity: if the top pain costs the owner less per year than your price, you have the wrong pain or the wrong segment.
- Every scope item must answer: which Value Equation term does this move? Delete items that move none.
- Put the guarantee condition on something you control the measurement of (response time, enquiries handled), not on their revenue.
- Name the package after the outcome and the segment, not after the technology.

## Anti-patterns
- Menu pricing (bronze/silver/gold) at this stage; it reintroduces comparison and option overload (see [09](09-the-jolt-effect.md)).
- Adding scope to justify price. Scope raises Effort and Time Delay for the buyer; it lowers value.
- Fake deadlines. Owners talk to each other; an invented "three slots left" costs trust that stage 05 cannot buy back.

## Worked Example
Package for owner-run studios: **"Every Enquiry Answered"**.
- Outcome: every Instagram/WhatsApp/web enquiry answered in under a minute and offered a trial slot, 7 days a week.
- Scope: enquiry agent built and tuned on their offers and FAQs; calendar integration; weekly lost/recovered report; 30-day tuning. Bonus: enquiry-to-trial script review for the desk team.
- Price: a fixed build fee plus a monthly fee, set against the value of one extra member per month at their average lifetime value.
- Terms: 50% to start, 50% at go-live; month-to-month after 90 days.
- Guarantee (conditional): if median first response exceeds two minutes in any week after go-live and they have supplied the agreed FAQ material, that month's fee is waived.

## Caveats
Hormozi's examples come from high-volume consumer and coaching offers; his urgency and scarcity advice conflicts with [09](09-the-jolt-effect.md), which found that pressure deepens indecision in considered B2B purchases, and with [05](05-the-trusted-advisor.md), where visible self-interest divides trust. This skill keeps his Value Equation and guarantee logic and drops manufactured urgency.
