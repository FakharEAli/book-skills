## Playbook 1 — Run a discovery call with a business owner
**Goal**: leave with a quantified gap in the owner's own numbers, an explicit need, O/R/D clear, and a dated Advance.
**Inputs**: company research (so Situation questions are minimal); your Problem Identification Chart for their vertical; the Advance you want.
**Steps**
1. Interrogative self-talk before dialling: "How will I find the problem behind what they ask for?" (08 Buoyancy).
2. Preliminaries ≤2 min: purpose, not credentials; read emotional state; reschedule if they are visibly stressed (01, 09).
3. Engage on what they said when booking; listen until "that's right" (03 Engage/Listen; 05).
4. If they open with a solution ("we want an AI assistant"), move off it: "What would that solve for you?" until a business result appears (10, 04).
5. Situation: ≤4 questions you could not research (01).
6. Problem → Evidence → Impact → Root cause → Emotion, in that order, in their numbers ("how do you know? how much? how often? why do you think that is? how does that feel on a Friday?") (01 P/I, 10, 04).
7. Collect every issue before diving on one; let them rank (10).
8. Name any yellow light as it appears (10, 05 label).
9. Need-payoff: "If that were fixed, what would it mean for you?" until they state a want (01).
10. If no impact surfaces: Challenger reframe from your PIC ("owners we work with think X; it's usually Y") then back to step 6 (06, 04). If still nothing: disqualify out loud (04, 10).
11. Resources and Decision: "Is there budget set aside, and in what range? Who else says yes, and what will they want to see?" No guessing (10).
12. Summarise current state → future state → gap in their words; aim for "that's right" (04, 05).
13. Ask for one spoken commitment and a dated Advance (data pull, stakeholder meeting) (01, 09).
**Output artifact**: Discovery note: Facts | Problems | Evidence | Impact (their numbers) | Root cause | Emotion | Future state | Gap statement | Resources | Decision process | Yellow lights named | Commitment + date.
**Common failure**: demonstrating capability at step 5 because they asked; value gets fixed low and every later conversation is about price.

## Playbook 2 — Rescue a stalled deal ("let me think about it")
**Goal**: classify the stall and apply the one lever that fits; set a decision date.
**Inputs**: discovery note; what has already been sent; the exact words of the stall.
**Steps**
1. Check: did they ever agree the problem is worth solving, in their numbers? If not, it is a status quo problem: go back to Implication/impact (01, 04, 09 why change / why now). Stop here; do not de-risk a problem they do not own.
2. If yes: label first. "It sounds like something's still not sitting right." Silence (05).
3. Ping to classify: "Is it whether to do this at all, which option, whether it'll work in your business, or who else needs to be on board?" Read the pong (02 Judge).
4. **Valuation** → recommend one path, remove the others, explain why in one sentence (02 Offer).
5. **Information** → "The three things that decide this for a business like yours are A, B, C. Here's where we stand on each. More research won't change the picture." Set a decision date (02 Limit).
6. **Outcome uncertainty** → shrink and de-risk: fixed-fee pilot, written exit criteria, named owner, lowered promised threshold you always beat (02 Take risk off; 07 expectation gap).
7. **Decision process** → "How does this get approved? What will they want to see?" Offer to present to them; tailor the insight to their scorecard (10, 05, 06 Tailor).
8. Inoculate the sceptic's best objection before the internal meeting (09).
9. Ask for a no-oriented commitment: "Would it be a bad idea to book the review for the 20th?" (05).
**Output artifact**: One-page "decision memo" for the buyer: the gap in their numbers · one recommendation · the three criteria and how you meet them · risk terms · decision date.
**Common failure**: sending another case study or ROI deck; it feeds the information loop and signals you did not hear the fear.

## Playbook 3 — Write the proposal and price it
**Goal**: a proposal the decider can say yes to without a meeting, priced to the gap, de-risked for an intangible service.
**Inputs**: discovery note with agreed gap; Results metric agreed (10); decision process; your position.
**Steps**
1. Do not write until Opportunity, Resources and Decision are real; if any is vague, go back and ask (10 ORDER).
2. Page one: restate current state → future state → gap in the buyer's numbers; no mention of your stack (04).
3. One recommendation, with a clearly secondary phase 2; no three-tier menu (02 Offer).
4. Describe the deliverable tangibly: named process with steps, diagram of systems touched, example output, named account owner (07).
5. Results section: the metric they named, the threshold you always beat, how and when it is reviewed (10 Results, 07 expectation).
6. Risk section: fixed fee, fixed length, exit criteria, what they keep if it stops (02 Take risk off).
7. Blemished frame: one honest limit ("it will not handle X; your team keeps that") (08).
8. Inoculate the two most likely objections in a short FAQ (09).
9. Price: anchor at the upper range justified by the gap; precise, non-round number; avoid the mushy middle; concessions pre-planned as scope or terms, not price (07, 05 Ackerman).
10. Matched proof: one case from a similar-sized business with numbers (09).
11. End with an off-ramp: one signature step and a date for the kickoff (08, 01 Advance).
**Output artifact**: Proposal skeleton: Gap (their numbers) → Recommendation → How it works (tangible) → Results & review → Risk terms → What it won't do → FAQ → Investment → Proof → Next step.
**Common failure**: leading with "About us" and a deliverables list; the buyer price-shops a document that never restated why they are buying.

## Playbook 4 — Negotiate scope and price without discounting
**Goal**: hold value, trade scope and terms, reach a precise agreement both sides own.
**Inputs**: your target, walk-away, pre-planned non-monetary concessions, the buyer's impact numbers.
**Steps**
1. Accusation audit if tension is likely: "You may be thinking this is expensive for a business your size and that I'll justify it with jargon" (05).
2. On the first price push: mirror and label, then a calibrated question: "What did the other quote cover for when it breaks on a Saturday?" Never answer a number with a number (05).
3. Return to the gap: their monthly cost of the problem vs the fee (04, 06 hold price).
4. Ask what matters most if budget is the constraint; cut scope to match, keeping the core result (10 Exact solution; 07 position).
5. Concede terms before price: longer support window, phased payments, pilot-first (05 non-monetary pivot).
6. If price must move: small, shrinking steps to a precise odd number; final step includes a non-monetary item and a clear "that's as far as I can go" (05 Ackerman).
7. Watch for "fair"; ask what feels unfair rather than defending (05).
8. Confirm in their words until "that's right"; book the kickoff (05, 01).
**Output artifact**: Negotiation prep card: Target | Walk-away | 3 non-monetary concessions in order | Scope-cut options | Their impact number | Accusation audit lines | Calibrated questions.
**Common failure**: splitting the difference in the meeting to end discomfort; you lack information and have just taught them that your price is soft.

## Playbook 5 — Position the offer and write landing-page / LinkedIn copy
**Goal**: one position, one reframe, visible safety, repeated to the same audience.
**Inputs**: your best vertical and outcome; three client stories with numbers; your process steps.
**Steps**
1. Position statement: who, what business, for whom, against what alternative, what is different; narrow to one line (07).
2. Choose the one reframe your clients did not know ("the bottleneck isn't X, it's Y") that leads to a capability only you deliver (06 Teach).
3. Landing page order: Warmer (their world) → Reframe → Rational drowning (one number) → Emotional impact (one story) → A New Way → Your solution (06 choreography).
4. Make it tangible: named process, diagram, annotated real output, named owner (07).
5. Fear reduction above the fold: pilot terms, exit criteria, what they keep (02, 07).
6. Blemished frame and one matched case (08, 09).
7. Off-ramp: a single low-risk next step ("send your line number; I'll count last week's missed calls") (08).
8. LinkedIn: one reframe per post, Pixar or question pitch, same position line every week to the same audience (08, 07 familiarity).
**Output artifact**: Position line · Reframe sentence · 6-block page outline · 4-week post calendar (one reframe each).
**Common failure**: "AI automation for any business" plus a feature list; no position, maximum fear.

## Playbook 6 — Cold outreach that earns a reply
**Goal**: a reply, not a meeting; one problem found, one easy step.
**Inputs**: a verifiable observation about the prospect; your PIC; one matched number.
**Steps**
1. Subject-line pitch: utility or curiosity plus specificity (08).
2. Open with the Warmer and the Reframe in two sentences; no introduction of yourself (06).
3. Question pitch that makes them compute their own impact (08, 01 Implication).
4. No-oriented ask or tiny off-ramp: "Would it be a bad idea to send you a count of…?" (05, 08).
5. Blemish: say what it will not do (08).
6. Follow-ups label, never nag: "It seems like this isn't a priority right now" (05); three touches, then stop.
7. Track replies per 50; explain silence as specific and temporary (08 Buoyancy).
**Output artifact**: Email template: Subject (specific) · Observation · Reframe · Question (their number) · Limit · One-step ask.
**Common failure**: "I'm the founder of an AI agency that helps businesses…" — self-oriented, no reframe, high fear.
