# Cheatsheet — decision rules, trees, thresholds, tells

## Decision rules
- When the buyer asks "so what do you do?" early → give a one-line position (07) and return to questions; presenting before an explicit need fixes value low (01).
- When the buyer arrives with a solution ("we need a chatbot") → move off the solution: "what would that solve?" until you reach a business result (10, 04).
- When discovery yields no quantified impact → stop selling, say so, offer to revisit; small gap = slow no (04, 10).
- When the buyer says "we're fine" → switch from SPIN to Challenger: Warmer → Reframe → Rational drowning (06).
- When you hear a pause, vague answer or incongruity → name it now (yellow light); do not push through (10, 05).
- When you do not know budget, timeline or decider → ask; never guess (10).
- When asked for a proposal before O/R/D are clear → decline politely and get them clear first (10).
- When presenting → one reframe, one recommendation, benefits mapped to stated explicit needs only (06, 02, 01).
- When the buyer must choose between options → recommend one; do not menu (02).
- When a buyer says "let me think about it" → run the JOLT classification ping before anything else (02); never add FOMO.
- When the buyer doubts the result will materialise → shrink and de-risk the first commitment; lower the promised number (02, 07).
- When you get a price objection → respond with a calibrated question, then return to the buyer's own impact numbers (05, 04, 09).
- When pressed to discount → cut scope or add non-monetary terms before touching price (05, 07).
- When a call ends → close for an Advance with a date; "send me info" is a Continuation (01, 09).
- When you win trust early → use it to challenge, not to coast (03 + 06).

## Decision tree: the stall
"Let me think about it" / silence / endless questions
- Have they agreed the problem is worth solving (why change, why now)?
  - **No** → status quo problem. Re-run Implication / impact / root cause with their numbers (01, 04, 09). Do *not* de-risk yet; there is nothing to de-risk.
  - **Yes** → indecision problem. Ping: "Which option, or whether it'll work here, or something else?"
    - **"Which option / too many choices"** → valuation. Recommend one; strip the menu (02 O).
    - **"Need to research / compare more"** → information. Name the 3 criteria that matter; say what more research will not change; set a decision date (02 L).
    - **"Not sure it'll work for us"** → outcome uncertainty. Fixed-fee pilot, exit criteria, lower promised threshold, named owner (02 T, 07).
    - **"Need to check with X"** → decision-process gap. "How does this get approved? What will X want to see?" Offer to join (10 D, 05).

## Decision tree: which questions now
- Buyer unaware of problem → Reframe first (06), then Problem questions (01).
- Buyer states a vague problem → Evidence ("how do you know?") → Impact ("how much?") → Root cause ("why?") (10, 04).
- Buyer states impact but no want → Need-payoff: "If that were fixed, what would it mean?" (01).
- Buyer states a want → stop asking; present one Benefit, then ask for the Advance (01).

## Trade-offs
| Choice | Prefer | Over | Because |
|---|---|---|---|
| Call time | Investigating | Demonstrating | value is built in the buyer's head (01) |
| Proof | one matched case with numbers | logo wall | similarity drives social proof (09) |
| Promise | threshold you always beat | best case | perception − expectation decides satisfaction (07, 02) |
| Offer shape | one recommendation + phase 2 | three tiers | tiers create a valuation problem (02) |
| Discount request | cut scope / add terms | cut price | price is positioning (07, 05) |
| Public position | one niche + one outcome | "custom AI for any business" | breadth reads as low expertise (07) |
| Objection reply | question | counter-argument | reactance; calibrated questions make them solve it (05) |
| Hesitation | name it now | handle at the end | surfaced early it is solvable (10) |
| Rapport | trust as licence to challenge | trust as the goal | Relationship Builders underperform in complex sales (06, 03) |

## Thresholds and defaults the authors commit to
- Situation questions: as few as research allows; 3–4 max in a discovery call (01).
- Pause after a label or mirror: ≥4 seconds (05).
- Ackerman steps: 65 → 85 → 95 → 100% of target; final figure precise and odd (05).
- "No decision" share of qualified pipeline: 40–60% (02, proprietary estimate).
- Commercial Teaching: solution appears only at step 6 of 6 (06).
- One reframe per pitch; one position per firm (06, 07).
- Every call ends with one spoken commitment and a date (01, 09).
- Pilot: fixed fee, fixed length, written exit criteria; never open-ended (02).

## Tells and smells
- "Send me something and I'll look" → Continuation; you have no Advance (01).
- "Budget is flexible" with no number → you are guessing; ask (10).
- Buyer asks for a fourth case study → lack-of-information indecision, not need for proof (02).
- Buyer says "you're right" → brush-off; you have not landed. "That's right" is the real signal (05).
- Buyer talks about "fair" → anchor emotions before numbers; ask what feels unfair (05).
- You are explaining your stack in the first 10 minutes → product-centric; stop (04).
- Three tiers on your proposal with equal weight → you built the valuation problem (02).
- Your proposal has no restated gap on page one → it will be price-shopped (04, 10).
- The owner mentions a previous vendor with a tight jaw → Intimacy and Reliability deficit; Engage/Listen before anything (03).
- Prospect agrees with everything and commits to nothing → test with a no-oriented question (05) and a small commitment ask (09).
- You feel the urge to split the difference → you lack information; ask a calibrated question instead (05).
- Discovery produced only hours saved, no revenue or risk impact → gap too small; dig or disqualify (04).
