## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

### Earn the right before you diagnose
**Trust Equation** (Maister, Green, Galford) — Trust = (Credibility + Reliability + Intimacy) / Self-orientation → Use when a prospect is guarded or burned by a past vendor. Prefer lowering Self-orientation (ask, listen, admit "I don't know") over adding Credibility (talking about your stack), because the divisor hits everything.
**Engage → Listen → Frame → Envision → Commit** (Maister et al.) — the order in which advice earns a hearing → Never Frame before the owner has said "that's right" to your summary.
**Tactical empathy: mirror, label, silence** (Voss) — repeat their last 1–3 words; "It sounds like…"; wait 4 seconds → Use at every yellow light and every price push; being heard precedes being persuaded.
**Attunement and interrogative self-talk** (Pink) — lower your status cues; ask "how will I find the real problem?" before the call → Prefer perspective-taking over empathy because it produces better outcomes for both sides.

### Build the gap in the buyer's own numbers
**SPIN** (Rackham) — Situation, Problem, Implication, Need-payoff; implied need → explicit need → Use Implication questions to grow the problem and Need-payoff questions so the buyer states the value. Present only against explicit needs, because Features raise price sensitivity and Advantages raise objections.
**Current state → Future state → The Gap** (Keenan) — facts, problems, impact, root cause, emotion → Use to size the deal before pricing. Prefer disqualifying a small gap over discounting into it, because gap size, not your rate card, sets willingness to pay.
**Move off the solution; Evidence / Impact / Context / Constraints; no guessing** (Khalsa, Illig) — "What would that solve for you?" until a business result appears; ask "how do you know?" before "how much?"; never guess budget or decider → Use when the lead arrives with a solution ("we need a chatbot").
**Commercial Teaching: Warmer → Reframe → Rational drowning → Emotional impact → A New Way → Your solution** (Dixon, Adamson) — → Use when discovery yields "we're fine"; lead to your unique capability, not with it, because a problem the buyer already knows is already priced.
**Advance vs Continuation** (Rackham) — end every call with a buyer action and a date → "Send me something" is a Continuation; treat it as a failed call.

### Diagnose the stall before you treat it
**Status quo vs indecision** (Dixon, McKenna) — two different losses with opposite cures → If the buyer has not owned the problem, build impact; if they have and still stall, stop selling change and make the choice safe. Adding FOMO to an indecisive buyer backfires because their fear is messing up.
**JOLT** (Dixon, McKenna) — Judge the indecision (ping and read the pong), Offer a recommendation, Limit the exploration, Take risk off the table → Classify the stall as valuation, information or outcome uncertainty and apply one lever. Prefer one recommendation plus phase 2 over three tiers, because tiers create the valuation problem.
**Six Whys** (Hoffeld) — why change, why now, why this category, why you, why this service, why spend → Map every objection to its unanswered why; "too expensive" is usually an unanswered why change or why now.
**Yellow lights** (Khalsa, Illig) — name hesitation the moment it appears → Unnamed hesitation becomes the end-of-call stall.

### Make the invisible safe to buy
**Fear of buying the invisible; make it tangible** (Beckwith) — service buyers avoid mistakes more than they seek gains → Show a named process, diagram, real output, named owner and exit terms. Prefer "safer and clearer" over "better", because the buyer cannot evaluate better.
**Perception − expectation** (Beckwith) and **set realistic expectations** (Dixon, McKenna) — promise the threshold you always beat → A credible 70% beats an exciting 90% the buyer doubts.
**One position** (Beckwith) and **one-word / question pitch** (Pink) — → "Lead-response automation for home-services companies", not "AI for any business".
**Buying commitments** (Hoffeld) — ask for one small spoken commitment per interaction → Momentum is a chain of public yeses.

### Negotiate without splitting the difference
**Calibrated questions and accusation audit** (Voss) — answer a price push with "What did the other quote cover for when it breaks?"; pre-state the worst they may think of you → Never answer a number with a number.
**Ackerman, inverted for the seller** (Voss) — anchor high, concede in shrinking steps to a precise odd number, finish with a non-monetary item → Use for price defence only; use Khalsa's co-design for scoping.
**Hold price, cut scope, pivot to terms** (Dixon & Adamson; Beckwith) — return to the quantified gap; trade support window or phasing before price, because price is positioning.
**Constructive tension** (Dixon, Adamson) — disagree with the belief, not the person, with data → Trust is the licence for tension, not a substitute for it.

## Scope & Limits
Distilled from the frameworks these books are known for, not from the book text. Where books disagree, the book files say so. Treat proprietary sales-research claims as practitioner evidence, not replicated science: SPIN (Huthwaite, ~35,000 calls), JOLT (Tethr, ~2.5M conversations) and Challenger (CEB survey) rest on proprietary, non-peer-reviewed data; Pink and Hoffeld cite psychology studies, several of which have replication problems.
