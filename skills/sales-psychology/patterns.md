# Patterns — every technique, deduplicated across the 10 books

Each pattern: what to do → why → source book(s). Where books overlap, the pattern is listed once with all sources.

## A. Before the conversation
- **Pre-map the problems you solve** (Problem Identification Chart): for each service line list symptoms, impacts, root causes → you cannot recognise a gap you have not mapped (04).
- **Set a commitment objective / target Advance** for the call: the specific buyer action you want (data shared, stakeholder meeting booked) → momentum is a chain of small, spoken commitments (01, 09).
- **Interrogative self-talk** ("How do I find the real problem here?") instead of affirmations → generates the plan (08).
- **Research the Situation facts** so you ask few Situation questions → they bore buyers and benefit only you (01).
- **Prepare Implication questions in writing** → hardest to improvise, most tied to success with senior buyers (01).
- **Prepare an accusation audit** for any tense message (price, scope cut, bad news) → pre-empted negatives lose their charge (05).

## B. Opening and earning the right
- **State purpose, not credentials** in the first two minutes → long Preliminaries lower success (01).
- **Engage on an issue they care about**, then **listen until "that's right"** before framing or advising → trust is the licence for everything after (03, 05).
- **Read emotional state first**; if negative, acknowledge or reschedule → selling into stress suppresses buying (09).
- **Lower your status cues** (sit back, slow down, ask) → power reduces perspective-taking (08).
- **Mirror and label** (repeat last 1–3 words; "It sounds like…") then stay silent ≥4s → being heard precedes being persuaded (05).
- **Warmer → Reframe** when the buyer thinks they are fine: lead with an insight about *their* business that only you can act on (06, 08 problem-finding).

## C. Discovery and building the gap
- **Move off the solution** the buyer arrived with: "What would that solve for you?" repeated to the business result (10, 04).
- **Question ladder: facts → problems → impact → root cause → emotion** (SPIN Situation/Problem/Implication; Gap Selling current state; Khalsa evidence/impact/context/constraints; Hoffeld facts → meaning → feeling) → the brain needs each layer to answer the next (01, 04, 09, 10).
- **Ask for evidence before impact** ("How do you know?") → impact on an unverified issue collapses later (10).
- **Quantify impact in the buyer's numbers**, never yours → their estimate is believed, yours is discounted (01, 04).
- **Dig to root cause** with "why do you think that is?" → a fix aimed at a symptom is judged a failure when the symptom returns (04).
- **Collect all issues before deep-diving one** → the first mentioned is rarely the most important (10).
- **Need-payoff: have the buyer state the value** ("If X were fixed, what would it mean?") → converts implied need to explicit need and rehearses their internal pitch (01).
- **Make the future state as concrete as the current state** → vague futures do not pull (04, 03 Envision).
- **Rational drowning after the reframe**: quantify why the reframed problem is bigger than they thought (06).
- **Name yellow lights immediately** ("You paused at X; what's behind that?") → unnamed hesitation becomes "let me think about it" (10, 05 label).
- **No guessing** on budget, timeline, decision process; ask, with permission if needed (10).
- **Map the decision process (ORDER: Decision) before any proposal** → the proposal is for the decider, not the contact (10, 05 "How does this get approved?").
- **Qualify on decisiveness**, not only need and budget → indecisive buyers with big needs are worse bets (02).
- **Disqualify out loud** when the gap is small or O/R/D stay vague → a slow no costs more than a fast one (04, 10).

## D. Presenting and positioning
- **Present Benefits only against explicit needs**; features raise price sensitivity, advantages raise objections (01).
- **One reframe, one position, one idea** per pitch or page → breadth reads as lack of expertise (06, 07, 08 one-word pitch).
- **Make the invisible tangible**: named process with steps, diagram, annotated real output, specific case numbers, named owner (07).
- **Recommend, do not menu**: "Given what you've told me, I'd start with X" → three equal tiers create a valuation problem (02).
- **Curate information**; three relevant facts beat a dossier (08, 02 limit exploration).
- **Blemished frame**: one honest limitation after the strengths → perfect offers read as sales copy (08).
- **Inoculate**: raise the competitor's or sceptic's best objection yourself and answer it (09).
- **Matched social proof** (same size, same industry) over big logos → similarity drives the effect (09).
- **Pixar / story for emotional impact** after the numbers → numbers earn the story (06, 08).
- **Question pitch** when your case is strong → the listener generates the reasons (08).
- **Give a "because"** with every ask (09).

## E. Handling stalls, objections and indecision
- **Diagnose before responding**: which of the Six Whys is unanswered? Status quo problem or indecision problem? (09, 02).
- **"Let me think about it" → ping to classify**: "Whether to do this at all, which option, or whether it'll work here?" → the pong tells you valuation / information / outcome uncertainty (02).
- **Valuation problem → recommend and remove options** (02).
- **Lack-of-information problem → limit the exploration**: name the three criteria that matter and say more research will not change the picture (02).
- **Outcome uncertainty → take risk off the table**: fixed-fee pilot, exit criteria, phased scope, keep-the-docs clause; *lower* the promised result to one you always beat (02, 07 expectation gap).
- **Never add FOMO to an indecisive buyer** → their dominant fear is messing up (02).
- **Price objection → calibrated question, not a number**: "What did the other quote include for when it breaks?" (05).
- **Return to why change / why now with their numbers** when the objection is really an unanswered earlier why (09, 04).
- **Label the fear** rather than argue with it (05, 03 Intimacy).
- **No-oriented questions** to unstick silence: "Have you given up on fixing this?" "Would it be a bad idea to…?" (05).
- **Constructive tension**: disagree with the belief, not the person; back it with data; let them respond (06).

## F. Negotiating scope and price
- **Anchor above target, concede in shrinking steps, end on a precise odd number plus a small non-monetary item** (Ackerman, inverted for the seller) (05).
- **Pivot to non-monetary terms** (support window, phasing, payment timing) before touching price (05).
- **Hold price by returning to the quantified gap**, not by defending cost (06, 04).
- **Reduce scope before reducing price** → protects positioning and the perception-minus-expectation gap (07, 02).
- **Price with intent**: avoid the mushy middle; no price resistance ever means too low (07).
- **Co-design the Exact solution and agree Results metrics before pricing** → price attaches to an outcome the buyer named (10).
- **Beware "fair"**: when invoked, stay calm and ask what feels unfair (05).

## G. Closing and after
- **Close for an Advance, never accept a Continuation**: a specific buyer action with a date (01, 09).
- **Off-ramp**: one easy, concrete next step in every message (08).
- **Make small promises and keep every one** → Reliability is the slowest trust term to build and the easiest to lose (03).
- **Set expectations you will visibly beat** (07, 02).
- **Serve test**: will their business be better if they say yes? If not, say so (08, 10, 03).
- **Explain rejection as temporary and specific** and keep prospecting (08).

## Where the books disagree
- **Relationship vs tension**: Challenger (06) says Relationship Builders lose; Trusted Advisor (03) and Khalsa (10) centre trust. Resolution: trust is the licence to create tension, not a substitute for it.
- **Ask vs tell**: SPIN/Gap/Khalsa (01, 04, 10) build value through questions; JOLT/Challenger (02, 06) insist you recommend and teach. Resolution: question until the gap is agreed, then recommend firmly.
- **Fear**: Beckwith (07) and JOLT (02) say reduce it; Challenger (06) rational drowning increases it. Resolution: raise the cost of the *problem*, lower the risk of the *solution*.
- **Haggling**: Voss (05) Ackerman is adversarial; Khalsa (10) is collaborative. Use Ackerman for price defence, Khalsa for scoping.
