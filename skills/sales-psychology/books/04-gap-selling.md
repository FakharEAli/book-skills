# 04 · Gap Selling
**Author(s)**: Keenan (Jim Keenan) | **Year**: 2018 | **Rank in category**: 04/10
**Why it's on the list**: current state, future state, the gap; problem-centric selling; root cause and impact

## Core Idea
Nobody buys a product; they buy a change from a current state they dislike to a future state they want. The distance between those two states is "the gap", and the size of the gap is the size of the sale. The seller's job is problem-centric: diagnose the current state in detail (facts, problems, impact, root cause, emotion), make the future state concrete, and let the gap itself create urgency. Selling without a clearly defined gap is guessing; a small or vague gap means either no deal or a price fight.

## Frameworks Introduced
**Current state → Future state → The Gap** — **Current state** has five components to uncover: the literal facts of the environment (tools, volumes, team), the problems (what is not working), the impact (what those problems cost in money, time, risk, morale), the root cause (why the problem exists), and the emotion (how the buyer feels about it). **Future state** mirrors it: the desired environment, problems removed, the impact of removal, the emotion. **The gap** is the delta; **How**: quantify it in the buyer's own numbers and state it back to them. **Why it works**: change is costly; buyers only pay when the gap is bigger than the pain of changing. **Failure mode**: accepting a surface problem ("we're slow") without impact and root cause produces a small gap and a commodity sale.

**The Problem Identification Chart (PIC)** — before prospecting, list the business problems your service actually solves, the symptoms a buyer would notice, the impacts, and the likely root causes. **How**: build one per service line; use it to prepare discovery questions and to disqualify prospects whose problems you do not solve. **Why**: you cannot recognise a gap you have not pre-mapped.

**Problem-centric vs product-centric selling** — product-centric starts from what you sell and looks for a place to put it; problem-centric starts from the buyer's problem and only mentions the product when it closes a specific gap. **How**: ban your product from the first conversation until the gap is on the table.

**Discovery as diagnosis** — probing with "how do you know?", "how much?", "how often?", "what happens when?", "why do you think that is?" to get from symptom to root cause and impact; the buyer is the expert on their business, you are the expert on the problem.

## Key Principles
- No problem, no sale: if discovery surfaces no quantified problem, stop selling and say so.
- Dig for root cause because a solution aimed at a symptom will be judged a failure when the symptom returns.
- Quantify impact in money, hours or risk, in the buyer's figures; your estimate is worth little, theirs is worth a lot.
- Make the future state as concrete as the current state; vague futures do not pull.
- The size of the gap, not your pricing, determines what the buyer will pay.
- Sell the change, not the tool: buyers resist new tools and accept getting out of a bad state.
- Hold next steps hostage to information: the proposal comes after the gap is agreed, never before.
- Disqualify early when the gap is small; a small gap is a slow no.

## Anti-patterns
- Pitching after one problem statement ("our follow-up is slow? we automate that!").
- Asking discovery questions you could have answered by research.
- Letting the buyer define the solution ("we want a chatbot") without finding the problem behind it.
- Proposals that describe your deliverables but never restate the gap.

## Worked Example (applied to Fakhar's business)
Prospect: an e-commerce brand owner who says "we need AI for customer service".
- **Facts**: 1,200 tickets a month, two agents on Gorgias, 11-hour median first response, 30% of tickets are "where is my order".
- **Problems**: slow responses, agents burned out, refunds granted to end arguments.
- **Impact** (ask, do not tell): "What does a chargeback cost you? How many refunds last month were given just to close the ticket? What's your repeat-purchase rate for customers who had a ticket vs those who didn't?" Owner estimates roughly 4% of revenue leaking.
- **Root cause**: no order-status integration, so humans answer lookups; no triage, so urgent tickets queue behind trivial ones.
- **Emotion**: "I'm embarrassed when I see the reviews."
- **Future state**: order-status answered instantly; agents handle only the 40% that need judgement; first response under 1 hour; refunds-to-close near zero; reviews mention service.
- **The gap, stated back**: "You're losing around 4% of revenue and your two best people to a problem that is mostly a missing integration plus triage. The future state you described removes most of that. That's the project, and 'AI customer service' is just how we get there."
- Only now: a scoped proposal that leads with the gap and quantifies it on page one.

## Caveats
- Based on Keenan's practitioner experience; no formal research base.
- Written in a blunt, sales-floor register; the method is sound but the tone can push owner-operators who want to be heard before being diagnosed (pair with 03 Trusted Advisor).
