# Patterns: every technique, deduplicated across the 10 books

Book numbers in brackets. Where several authors describe the same move, the pattern is listed once with all sources.

## A. Before the message: arranging attention
- **Make the problem focal first** — open with a diagnostic question or specific number about *their* process before any mention of you; what is focal is judged important and causal (04). Pairs with Simple/Commander's Intent (05).
- **Self-description opener** — ask a question whose honest answer is the identity your offer serves ("the kind of owner who fixes it once"); people act consistently with what they just said (04, 01 commitment).
- **Pre-ask** — "If I could show you X, would you want to see it?" before showing it (04); a small freely-chosen commitment (01).
- **Prime the environment** — audit imagery, metaphors and vocabulary on site, deck and booking page; remove cues of cost/complexity/risk, add cues of control/calm/done (04, 03 signals).
- **Unfinished hook** — open content with a counterintuitive, incomplete statement about the reader's situation; resolve after the core idea (04 mystery, 05 Unexpected/knowledge gap).
- **Trigger-timed contact** — reach owners at life-event moments (new hire, new system, new premises, funding) when habits are loose (02 habit, 02 FAE, 07 "burn the ships"), and at recurring cues (month-end, Monday) (06 Triggers, 10 category entry points).

## B. Perception, framing and value
- **Net value = reward − pain** — enumerate every pain (price, effort, risk, time, learning, lock-in) and remove or reframe each before touching price (03). Uncertainty is the biggest hidden pain (07).
- **Sell the implicit goal** — diagnose whether the owner buys security/autonomy/discipline or excitement/adventure; keep every signal consistent (03). Mismatched goal = low value on autopilot.
- **Reference-point pricing** — never a bare number; price beside the cost of the problem or beside a premium tier (02 price relativity, 01 contrast, 06 reference points). Rule of 100: pounds for savings over 100, percent under (06).
- **Reduce pain of payment** — one monthly figure, outcomes not hours, no itemised ledger on first view (02).
- **Price as signal** — a confident price raises expected and experienced quality; discounting signals doubt (02 expectancy, 02 Veblen, 03, 08).
- **Costly signalling** — make confidence expensive to fake: refund guarantee, hand-built free audit, named engineer; cheap claims are discounted to zero (08, 09 trustworthiness).
- **Reframe the metric** — fix the anxiety around the number (visibility, check-ins, kill-switch) when fixing the number is expensive (08).
- **Doorman check** — before automating a human step, list its hidden functions (judgement, relationship, reassurance) and preserve them (08).
- **Name the thing** — a named programme/method is trusted and bought; an unnamed service is compared on price (08, 05 Concrete).
- **Distinctive, not just different** — fix one identity (colour, diagram style, face, sign-off, tagline) for years; buyers recognise before they compare (10, 03 autopilot cues, 02 distinctiveness).

## C. Making it stick (content and copy)
- **One core, compactly** — Commander's Intent for every page and post; if they remember one thing (05 Simple). Primacy: strongest number first (02).
- **Filmable concreteness** — replace every abstraction with something a camera could capture (05 Concrete, 03 fluency).
- **Human-scale statistics** — convert hours to "one admin person"; the one over the many (05 Credible, Emotional).
- **Sinatra Test credential** — one proof that implies the rest (05).
- **Testable credential** — let them try it: paste-your-process audit, demo on their data (05; also trialability 07).
- **Ban semantic stretch** — strike "AI-powered", "seamless", "cutting-edge", "innovative" (05).
- **Challenge-plot client stories with you as the hinge** — the story must be untellable without your intervention (05 Stories, 06 Trojan Horse / valuable virality).
- **High-arousal emotion** — lead with absurdity, awe, anger or relief; not calm satisfaction (06 Emotion).
- **Narrow practical value** — checklists and benchmarks for one segment; narrow usefulness gets forwarded (06).
- **Social currency line** — one repeatable, surprising fact per piece (06).
- **Make results public** — named cases, shared dashboards, tagged posts, behavioural residue (06 Public, 01 social proof).
- **Cocktail-party specificity** — name the segment in the first line (02).

## D. Proof, trust and the messenger
- **Similar social proof** — proof from firms of the same size and sector; dissimilar logos fail the translation problem (01, 07 Corroborating Evidence, 02).
- **Positive, quantified adoption** — "61 of 90 agencies already..."; never negative social proof (01, 02).
- **Stack, concentrate, diversify proof** — several independent, similar sources inside one week for strong resistance; one strong source for weak (07).
- **Authority via third party** — have the credential delivered by a referrer, headline or case, not asserted (01, 09 association).
- **Admit a limit early** — a true flaw before the strength; trustworthiness by apparent disinterest (01 authority, 02 pratfall, 09 vulnerability/trustworthiness). Only after competence is shown (09).
- **Recommend against yourself once** — the smaller option, or "don't buy yet", when true (09, 01).
- **Demonstrate, don't claim, competence** — do a small piece of the work live (09, 05 testable).
- **Sell potential as well as record** — the 90-day picture (09 competence).
- **Match messenger to context** — decisive/dominant when the owner is anxious and asking for direction; warm/collaborative when exploring (09).
- **Advice, not opinion** — ask prospects for advice on your offer to create a "together" state (01 unity).
- **Give first, specifically** — a personalised diagnostic, not a generic consultation (01 reciprocity).

## E. Removing resistance (the REDUCE set, 07, with allies)
- **Diagnose the barrier before responding** — reactance, endowment, distance, uncertainty, or evidence; each has a different fix (07).
- **Menu of 2–3 scoped options** — choice lowers reactance (07); also contrast (01).
- **Ask, don't tell; understand first** — restate their position before offering yours (07, 04).
- **Highlight the say-do gap** — what they say they value vs the current process (07).
- **Surface the cost of inaction** — hours × weeks × salary, cumulative (07 Endowment, 03 pain).
- **Shrink the ask** — first workflow, not the roadmap; stay inside the zone of acceptance (07 Distance).
- **Switch the field** — move the conversation to a dimension they already agree on ("stop missing invoices", not "adopt AI") (07).
- **Trialability and reversibility** — pilot, fixed fee, old process left intact, cancel monthly, refund (07 Uncertainty, 08 costly signal).
- **Real scarcity only** — capacity or dates that are true; stated as loss; use after barriers are down (01, 07).

## F. Growth system (10, with allies)
- **Mental availability by situation** — map 6–10 buying situations and attach the brand to each as a recurring theme (10, 06 Triggers).
- **Continuous reach to the whole segment** — not only ideal-customer targeting; light buyers are the future clients (10).
- **Consistent yet fresh** — same assets and themes, new examples (10).
- **Frictionless buying** — public pricing, booking link everywhere, fast reply (10 physical availability, 03 behavioural cost).
- **Remove reasons not to buy** — jargon, odd pricing, unclear scope (10).
- **Test before you believe** — cheap A/B on openers, prices, hooks; claimed preferences are unreliable (02, 08).

## Where the books disagree
- **Differentiation**: Sharp (10) says buyers barely perceive it; Barden (03), Berger (07) and the Heaths (05) show mechanism and fit matter in considered purchases. For B2B services, side with 03/07 on the proposal and with 10 on brand consistency.
- **Push vs remove**: Cialdini (01) supplies levers that push; Berger (07) says remove barriers first. Order: 07 then 01.
- **Logic**: Sutherland (08) distrusts spreadsheets; Shotton (02) and Sharp (10) insist on testing and data. Resolve: test counterintuitive ideas with data.
