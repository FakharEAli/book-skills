# 05 · Made to Stick: Why Some Ideas Survive and Others Die
**Author(s)**: Chip Heath, Dan Heath | **Year**: 2007 | **Rank in category**: 05/10
**Why it's on the list**: SUCCESs: Simple, Unexpected, Concrete, Credible, Emotional, Stories; curse of knowledge

## Core Idea
Ideas that stick (are understood, remembered and change behaviour) share six traits, summarised as **SUCCESs**. The main obstacle to producing them is the **Curse of Knowledge**: once you know something, you cannot imagine not knowing it, so experts talk in abstractions that mean nothing to the audience. Sticky communication is a discipline of finding the core and then making it concrete enough that a novice can picture it.

## Frameworks Introduced
**SUCCESs** — the six-trait checklist.
- **Simple**: find the *core* (the one thing that matters most) and express it *compactly*. Tools: **Commander's Intent** (the one outcome everything else serves); forced prioritisation ("if they remember one thing"); proverbs and **generative analogies** that let people derive further implications ("a Disney employee is a cast member"). **Failure**: burying the lead; three equal messages.
- **Unexpected**: break a pattern to get attention, then open a **knowledge gap** (the gap theory of curiosity) to hold it. **How**: state the counterintuitive fact, then make the audience aware of what they don't know before you fill it. **Failure**: gimmick surprise that doesn't relate to the core.
- **Concrete**: sensory, specific, picture-able. The **Velcro theory of memory**: more concrete hooks, more recall. **How**: replace every abstraction ("efficiency", "optimisation") with a thing someone could film. **Failure**: the curse of knowledge speaking.
- **Credible**: *external* (authorities and **anti-authorities**, i.e. ordinary people with lived experience), *internal* (vivid details, statistics made **human-scale**, the **Sinatra Test**: one credential that proves everything, "if it can make it there..."), and **testable credentials** ("try it yourself"). **Failure**: big numbers without a human reference.
- **Emotional**: make people care by appealing to **self-interest** (WIIFY: what's in it for you), to **identity**, and to the **one** rather than the many (the Mother Teresa principle: a single identifiable person moves people more than a statistic). Avoid **semantic stretch**, overused words that have lost force ("innovative", "AI-powered"). **Failure**: appealing to abstract benefits the audience can't feel.
- **Stories**: stories *simulate* (mental rehearsal of how to act) and *inspire*. Three sticky plots: **Challenge** (underdog overcomes), **Connection** (bridging a gap between people), **Creativity** (a mental breakthrough). **Failure**: a brand story about you rather than a customer story they can see themselves in.

**Curse of Knowledge** — the expert's inability to reconstruct the novice's view. **How**: explain to a specific non-expert and watch where they frown; use their words, not yours.

## Key Principles
- Decide the one thing a business owner must remember from your site, because three messages are zero messages.
- Lead with the core, because the first line is the only line most will read (also 02 primacy).
- Write so a thing could be filmed: "the invoice goes out at 5pm on the day the job closes" beats "automated billing workflows".
- Open a knowledge gap before answering it, because curiosity holds attention through a long post.
- Make statistics human-scale: "that's one full-time admin, every week" beats "40 hours/week saved".
- Choose one named customer over a logo wall, because the one moves people and the many are a statistic.
- Pass the Sinatra Test with a single credential that implies the rest ("we run the intake system for a 200-person law firm").
- Give a testable credential: a self-serve audit, a demo on their data.
- Tell Challenge stories about clients, because they simulate the prospect's own journey.
- Ban semantically stretched words from your copy ("AI-powered", "seamless", "cutting-edge").

## Anti-patterns
- Feature-first copy ("n8n, OpenAI, vector search"), which is the curse of knowledge made public.
- Surprise without a core (clickbait hook, generic body).
- Abstract benefits ("drive efficiency") that no one can picture or feel.
- Founder-journey LinkedIn posts with no Connection to the reader's problem.

## Worked Example (applied to Fakhar's business)
Homepage hero rewrite.
- **Core (Commander's Intent)**: "Your back office runs itself."
- **Unexpected hook**: "Most firms don't lose time doing the work. They lose it handing the work over."
- **Concrete**: "A booking lands at 9:02. By 9:03 the customer has a confirmation, the job is in the schedule, and the invoice is drafted. Nobody touched it."
- **Credible**: Sinatra Test: "We run intake for a 200-person law firm." Human-scale statistic: "Our clients get back roughly one admin person per 20 staff." Testable: "Paste your current process below; we'll send a map of what to automate first."
- **Emotional (identity)**: "For owners who'd rather build the business than babysit it."
- **Story (Challenge)**: 4-line case: "Dani ran a 15-person cleaning company and was approving every invoice by hand at 11pm. Six weeks in, invoices approve themselves against the job sheet. She found out it worked when she noticed she'd stopped checking."

## Caveats
SUCCESs is a synthesis of research on memory and communication, not a tested model itself; the strongest evidence is for concreteness and the identifiable-victim effect. "Unexpected" is easy to over-apply into gimmickry. The Heaths wrote for communicators generally; B2B buyers also need the mechanism to be *believable* (07) and the price frame to be right (03).
