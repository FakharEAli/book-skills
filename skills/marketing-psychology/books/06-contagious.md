# 06 · Contagious: Why Things Catch On
**Author(s)**: Jonah Berger | **Year**: 2013 | **Rank in category**: 06/10
**Why it's on the list**: STEPPS: Social currency, Triggers, Emotion, Public, Practical value, Stories

## Core Idea
Word of mouth is the main driver of adoption for most products and services, and most of it happens offline, in ordinary conversation. Things get talked about not because of budgets or "influencers" but because of six properties of the message itself, which Berger summarises as **STEPPS**. Design the content and the offer so that sharing it makes the sharer look good, is prompted by everyday cues, and carries the brand inside a story worth retelling.

## Frameworks Introduced
**STEPPS** — the six drivers of word of mouth.
- **Social Currency**: people share what makes them look smart, in-the-know or high-status. Tools: **inner remarkability** (surprising facts), **game mechanics** (visible status, levels, leaderboards), **making people feel like insiders** (access, scarcity, exclusivity). **How**: give owners a fact or result they can repeat at a dinner table and sound sharp.
- **Triggers**: "top of mind, tip of tongue." Things get talked about when the environment cues them. **How**: link the offer to a frequent, specific cue in the buyer's week (Monday admin, month-end invoicing, the moment a new hire asks "where's the process?"). Grow the **habitat**: more cues, more mentions. **Why it works**: immediate word of mouth is interest; ongoing word of mouth is triggers.
- **Emotion**: "when we care, we share." **High-arousal** emotions (awe, excitement, amusement, anger, anxiety) drive sharing; **low-arousal** ones (sadness, contentment) suppress it. **How**: focus on feelings, not functions; find the awe or the anger in the problem (the admin who spent 400 hours a year re-typing data).
- **Public**: "built to show, built to grow." Private behaviour cannot be imitated. Tools: **behavioural residue** (artefacts left behind after use), making the private visible. **How**: dashboards clients screenshot, a "runs on [agency]" badge in their Slack, public case studies with the client's name.
- **Practical Value**: "news you can use." People share useful, specific, narrowly targeted content. The **Rule of 100**: under 100, state discounts as a percentage; over 100, as an absolute amount. Value is judged against **reference points**. **How**: publish checklists, benchmarks, step-by-step fixes; narrow the audience so forwarding feels like a personal favour.
- **Stories**: information travels inside narratives (the **Trojan Horse**). **Valuable virality**: the brand must be integral to the story, or people retell the story and drop the brand. **How**: client stories where the mechanism of the result is your work, so the story cannot be told without you.

## Key Principles
- Give every piece of content one repeatable, surprising line, because people share what makes them look informed.
- Attach the offer to a recurring moment in the buyer's week, because the trigger, not the ad, produces ongoing mentions.
- Lead with high-arousal emotion (the absurdity of the manual process, the relief of it gone), because calm contentment does not get forwarded.
- Make results visible to the client's peers (named case studies, shared dashboards, LinkedIn tags), because visible behaviour is the only kind that spreads.
- Publish narrowly useful content ("the 5 n8n workflows every recruitment agency needs"), because narrow usefulness is what gets forwarded to the one person it fits.
- Apply the Rule of 100 to any saving you quote: "£1,100 a month" not "42%".
- Build stories where your intervention is the hinge, because otherwise the story spreads and you don't.
- Design for offline word of mouth (what one owner says to another at a networking breakfast), because that is where most of it happens.

## Anti-patterns
- Content that is interesting but untriggered; it gets one burst and dies.
- "Brand storytelling" where the brand could be removed without changing the story.
- Private wins: a client happy in silence spreads nothing.
- Broad "tips for all businesses" content that is useful to everyone and forwarded by no one.

## Worked Example (applied to Fakhar's business)
LinkedIn content system for the agency.
- **Social Currency**: a weekly "hand-off audit" post with one counterintuitive finding ("the slowest step in most sales processes is the CRM update, not the call").
- **Triggers**: posts timed to month-end and Monday morning; copy references the moment ("it's the 28th; someone in your office is chasing invoices right now").
- **Emotion**: open with the absurd or the relieving, not the technical ("she had 11 browser tabs open just to onboard one client").
- **Public**: every delivery closes with a client-tagged post and a screenshot of the live dashboard; a one-line "automated by" footer in client-facing emails where the client agrees.
- **Practical Value**: monthly downloadable checklist, segmented by industry; savings quoted in pounds.
- **Stories**: Challenge-plot client stories (05) where the turning point is the specific workflow you built.

## Caveats
STEPPS was derived from analyses of viral content and consumer products; B2B word of mouth is lower volume and more trust-gated (09). "Public" has limits in professional services where clients prefer confidentiality; ask first. The Rule of 100 is a heuristic, not a law.
