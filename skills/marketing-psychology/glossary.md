# Glossary

Term → one-line definition → (book ##)

- **Anti-authority** → an ordinary person with lived experience whose testimony is credible precisely because they are not an expert → (05)
- **Attention = importance** → whatever is focal is judged more important and more causal than it is → (04)
- **Autopilot / Pilot** → Barden's names for the implicit fast system that drives most choices and the explicit slow system that justifies them → (03)
- **Behavioural residue** → visible traces left after a behaviour that keep advertising it (stickers, badges, dashboards) → (06)
- **Burn the ships** → removing the option of staying with the status quo when the old way is about to break anyway → (07)
- **Category entry points** → the cues and situations in which a buyer thinks of the category; mental availability is built on them → (10)
- **Claimed data** → self-reported reasons and preferences, which are unreliable guides to behaviour → (02)
- **Cocktail party effect** → we notice our own name or group amid noise; specificity grabs attention → (02)
- **Commander's Intent** → the single outcome everything else serves; the core of a Simple message → (05)
- **Commitment and consistency** → once a stand is taken, people act in line with it; strongest when active, public, effortful, freely chosen → (01)
- **Contrast principle** → judgements shift relative to what was just presented → (01)
- **Corroborating Evidence** → multiple similar, independent sources concentrated in time, needed to overcome the translation problem → (07)
- **Costly signalling** → a message is credible in proportion to how expensive it would be to fake → (08)
- **Curse of Knowledge** → once you know something you cannot imagine not knowing it, so you communicate in abstractions → (05, 02)
- **Distance** → the gap between a message and the audience's current position; too far falls in the region of rejection → (07)
- **Distinctive brand assets** → recognisable non-name elements (colour, logo, tagline, face) that trigger the brand → (10)
- **Distinctiveness vs differentiation** → being recognised vs being different; Sharp argues the first drives choice, the second barely registers → (10)
- **Doorman fallacy** → reducing a role to its narrow function and losing its unmeasured value when you automate or remove it → (08)
- **Double jeopardy** → smaller brands have fewer buyers who are also slightly less loyal → (10)
- **Endowment** → over-valuing what one already has; the status quo bias barrier → (07)
- **Expectancy theory** → expectations (set by price, brand, framing) change the experience itself → (02)
- **Framing effect / brand as frame** → the same offer is valued differently inside a different frame of signals → (03)
- **Fundamental attribution error** → over-crediting personality and under-crediting context; target contexts, not personas → (02)
- **Gap theory of curiosity** → attention is held by an opened knowledge gap awaiting closure → (05)
- **Generative analogy** → an analogy from which the audience can derive further correct implications → (05)
- **Hard messengers** → status-based traits: socio-economic position, competence, dominance, attractiveness → (09)
- **Implicit goals** → psychological goals a purchase serves: security, enjoyment, excitement, adventure, autonomy, discipline → (03)
- **Light buyers** → infrequent category buyers who make up the majority and drive growth → (10)
- **Mental availability** → the probability of being noticed or thought of in a buying situation → (10)
- **Movable middle** → people already part-way to your position, where persuasion effort pays → (07)
- **Mystery story** → a counterintuitive puzzle whose delayed resolution holds attention → (04)
- **Negative social proof** → publicising how many people do the undesired behaviour, which normalises it → (01, 02)
- **Net value** → perceived reward minus perceived pain (price plus behavioural cost) → (03)
- **Pain of payment** → the discomfort of paying, driven by salience more than amount → (02)
- **Physical availability** → being easy to find and buy at the moment of need → (10)
- **Pratfall effect** → a competent person who shows a flaw becomes more likeable and credible → (02, 09)
- **Pre-suasion** → arranging attention before the message so the audience is receptive when it arrives → (04)
- **Price relativity** → prices are judged against reference points the seller can supply → (02)
- **Privileged moment** → a brief window of heightened receptivity created by what was just focal → (04)
- **Psycho-logic** → the consistent but non-economic rules behind human choices → (08)
- **Reactance** → resistance triggered by feeling pushed; the anti-persuasion radar → (07, 01)
- **Reciprocity** → the urge to repay what one receives, including concessions → (01)
- **REDUCE** → Reactance, Endowment, Distance, Uncertainty, Corroborating Evidence: the five barriers to change → (07)
- **Rejection-then-retreat** → a large request refused, then a smaller one accepted as a reciprocated concession → (01)
- **Rule of 100** → state discounts as a percentage under 100 and as an absolute amount over 100 → (06)
- **Scarcity** → less available seems more valuable; strongest when newly scarce and under competition → (01)
- **Semantic stretch** → words overused until they carry no force ("innovative", "seamless") → (05)
- **Signals / diagnostic cues** → visible features from which the autopilot infers unseen quality → (03)
- **Sinatra Test** → one credential that proves everything ("if it can make it there") → (05)
- **Social Currency** → sharing that makes the sharer look good → (06)
- **Social proof** → deciding what is correct by what similar others do, especially under uncertainty → (01)
- **Soft messengers** → connection-based traits: warmth, vulnerability, trustworthiness, charisma → (09)
- **STEPPS** → Social Currency, Triggers, Emotion, Public, Practical Value, Stories: the drivers of word of mouth → (06)
- **SUCCESs** → Simple, Unexpected, Concrete, Credible, Emotional, Stories: the traits of sticky ideas → (05)
- **Testable credential** → a claim the audience can verify for themselves → (05)
- **Translation problem** → "it worked for them, but they're not me"; why dissimilar proof fails → (07)
- **Trialability** → ability to try before committing; lowers the uncertainty tax → (07)
- **Triggers** → environmental cues that bring a product to mind ("top of mind, tip of tongue") → (06)
- **Trojan Horse story** → a narrative that carries the brand inside it so the brand travels with the story → (06)
- **Uncertainty tax** → the cost people assign to a new, unproven option, leading them to wait → (07)
- **Unity** → shared identity ("we") built by being together and acting together; the seventh principle → (01)
- **Valuable virality** → sharing that carries the brand, not just the story → (06)
- **Velcro theory of memory** → the more concrete hooks an idea has, the more it sticks → (05)
- **Zone of acceptance / region of rejection** → the range of positions a person will consider vs reject outright → (07)
