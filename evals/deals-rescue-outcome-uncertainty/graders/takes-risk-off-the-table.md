# Grader: uses Take risk off the table with concrete terms

The matching JOLT lever is Take risk off the table. Ravi's offer already contains real terms: phased delivery with the option to stop after Phase 1 (£3,200, 2 weeks), and support with a 4-business-hour weekday response, a daily health check and Ravi as named contact. On the call he answered her fear with reassurance ("It won't break, honestly", "It'll be fine"), which is the mistake to correct.

**PASS** if the primary message draft:
- Leads with or clearly centres on de-risking, using at least two concrete terms among: a phased start (Phase 1 quote follow-up only) with the right to stop after it; support terms (named contact, response time, daily health check); a manual fallback ("if it stops, Kerry does exactly what she does today"); a pilot or exit clause, and
- States those terms specifically (numbers, durations, who), matching the offer as given. Any term not in the offer (e.g. a pilot, a money-back guarantee, free first month) is flagged for Ravi's approval (e.g. `[NEEDS APPROVAL]`) or presented to Ravi as an option, not promised to Dana as settled, and
- Addresses Kerry's "not technical" worry or the Monday-morning failure fear directly, and
- Optionally sets realistic expectations rather than bigger promises.

**FAIL** if any of:
- The message relies on reassurance ("it's really robust", "you won't have any problems") or more information (case studies, a longer demo, an ROI deck) as the main move.
- It invents a guarantee, pilot or refund and presents it to Dana as already on offer without flagging it.
- It invents client results or testimonials (Ravi has none).
- It uses more than one JOLT lever as the main move (e.g. a menu of options plus criteria plus pilot).
