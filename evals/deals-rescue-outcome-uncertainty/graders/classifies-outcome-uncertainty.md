# Grader: diagnoses indecision from outcome uncertainty, not status quo

Dana owns the problem in her own numbers ("We probably lose eight, ten grand a month on quotes nobody chases", "The quotes thing is killing us though, I know that"), so this is not a status-quo stall. Her stated fear is that it fails in use: "what if it breaks on a Monday morning and my team can't use it?", the whiteboard story, Kerry "stuck with something she can't use". Under JOLT (Dixon & McKenna) that is indecision driven by outcome uncertainty.

**PASS** if the response:
- Classifies the stall as indecision / outcome uncertainty (fear it won't work for them, fear of messing up), and
- Supports the classification with at least one of Dana's or Kerry's quotes about breaking, not being able to use it, or the previous software, and
- Rules out status quo by pointing to Dana's stated impact (or equivalent reasoning that she already agrees the problem is worth solving).

**FAIL** if any of:
- The diagnosis is status quo / lack of urgency / "she isn't convinced of the problem", and the main move is to build more business case or ROI.
- The diagnosis is valuation (too many options) or lack of information as the primary type.
- The deal is treated as simply "busy, needs a nudge" with no diagnosis.
