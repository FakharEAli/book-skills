# Grader: justifies the format by awareness stage, with evidence from the notes

**PASS** if all of these hold:
- It names the segment's awareness stage as Unaware (or "problem-unaware"), using Schwartz's stages of awareness or an equivalent explicit awareness model, by name.
- It cites at least two of the fixture's owner statements (quoted or closely referenced, e.g. "Our phones are fine", "I put it down to the season") as evidence for that stage.
- It explains the mismatch: a calculator or ROI model asks the owner to quantify a problem they do not yet accept, so it suits problem-aware or product-aware buyers, while a scorecard lets an unaware owner find the problem for themselves.

**FAIL** if any of these hold:
- No awareness stage is named, or the stage is given as problem-aware, solution-aware or higher without addressing the fixture's evidence.
- The format is justified only by generic reasons ("quizzes convert well", "people love scorecards") with no link to the segment's awareness.
- It invents survey data or percentages about the owners to justify the stage.
